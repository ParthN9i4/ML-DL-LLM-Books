42.1 How to Read This Literature

Section 40.5's five-point latency checklist is a special case. The general problem is that a paper here makes claims in three currencies at once — cryptographic, systems, machine-learning — and a reviewer strong in one is routinely weak in the other two, which is why Section 42.2's overclaims survive peer review. Extract the following before forming any opinion, in an order chosen so the earliest points invalidate the most papers.

Definition 42.1 (The nine-point extraction)

For any private-inference paper, record: (1) Threat model — who is hidden from whom, who must stay online, and whether the model weights are plaintext at the server. (2) The function actually evaluated — the architecture after surgery, not the one in the title; a distilled, softmax-free, LayerNorm-linearized network is a different function from the checkpoint it is named after. (3) Depth ledger — levels per block, bootstraps per inference, and whether either is reported at all. (4) Packing and slot utilization — what fraction of the $N/2$ slots carry live data at the narrowest point of the circuit. (5) The baseline — which system, rerun by whom, at whose parameters. (6) The timing boundary — key generation, weight encoding, client compute, and network time are each either inside or outside; papers differ. (7) The accuracy bill — task, metric, and the plaintext reference it is measured against. (8) Amortization convention — per input, or per slot-batch of inputs that no real deployment can assemble. (9) Reproducibility — library and version, ring dimension, level budget, and claimed security level with the estimator used.

Points 3 and 4 are the ones a machine-learning reader skips and should not. A paper reporting end-to-end latency but no depth ledger has made its central design decision — where to bootstrap — unauditable. Point 4 catches the most common silent inefficiency: a system that packs activations into ciphertexts of $N/2 = 32{,}768$ slots and then evaluates a per-head operation touching $d_k = 64$ slots at a time runs at $0.2\%$ utilization, and its rotation counts will be enormous for reasons unrelated to the idea being proposed. Likely

Point 9 is where cryptographic and machine-learning norms genuinely conflict. CKKS is an approximate scheme, and Li and Micciancio (EUROCRYPT 2021; ePrint 2020/1533) showed the standard IND-CPA notion is insufficient for it: decryption returns an approximation whose error is key-dependent, so an adversary seeing decryptions of known computations can recover the secret key — demonstrated in practice against HEAAN, SEAL, HElib, and PALISADE. The repaired notion is IND-CPA$^{\mathrm{D}}$; the countermeasure is flooding the decryption output with key-independent noise. Certain The systems consequence is concrete: flooding costs precision, precision costs polynomial degree, degree costs depth — so a latency number measured without the countermeasure is measuring a cheaper scheme. Few machine-learning-venue papers here state which side of that line they are on.

Common Pitfall

Comparing your system's accuracy against the original checkpoint's published GLUE score rather than against your own plaintext run of your own modified architecture. The failure is not subtle: if you replace softmax with a degree-15 polynomial and LayerNorm with a learned affine map, you must report the plaintext accuracy of that network as the reference, and separately report the encrypted-versus-plaintext gap of the same network. Papers that conflate the two attribute architectural damage to the cryptography, or — far more often — hide architectural damage inside a claim of "no accuracy loss under encryption." Both numbers are needed; either alone is uninterpretable.

42.2 What Is Routinely Overclaimed

Five claims recur, in rough order of how often they appear.

"Private." Unquantified, it almost always means "the input tensor is not sent in the clear." It rarely means the output distribution, the sequence length (ciphertext counts reveal it), or the architecture is hidden, or that a client-aided decryption step leaks nothing — the criticism Chapter 38 recorded against client-aided designs. The settling experiment: a simulation-based leakage statement, exhibiting a simulator that produces the server's view from public parameters and input shape alone; failing that, an empirical attack reconstructing inputs from what the server does observe.

Speedup factors. Every headline "$k\times$ faster" is against a baseline the authors reran, at parameters they chose, on hardware the baseline's authors never used; Example 40.1 put the resulting noise floor near $1.5\times$. The settling experiment: a shared artifact benchmark — fixed model, sequence length, ring dimension and level budget, one machine, all systems rerun by a third party. The field has none, which makes it the most valuable unglamorous contribution available. Likely

"Accuracy is preserved." Usually established on sentence classification at sequence length 128, where a two-class boundary tolerates enormous representational damage. The settling experiment: perplexity of the polynomial surrogate on a held-out corpus, same tokenizer, reported beside the plaintext base — Chapter 29's discipline applied to encrypted models. Losing $0.3\%$ on SST-2 and four points of perplexity is not accuracy preservation.

"Scales to a 7B/8B model." Chapter 41 took this apart: one forward pass over an 8-token prompt is not generation, and per-token cost under a growing encrypted cache is a staircase. The settling experiment: generate 128 tokens; report the latency curve and the text.

Security-level claims. "128-bit security" attached to a parameter set chosen for depth rather than checked against a current lattice estimator, silent on IND-CPA$^{\mathrm{D}}$. The settling experiment: print the estimator output and the flooding parameter. Certain

None of this makes the literature dishonest; it makes it young. The venues that would enforce measurement conventions (security) and those that understand the models (machine learning) are disjoint.

42.3 The SSM Opportunity: Depth Arithmetic

Chapter 21 built selective state-space models: linear-in-$T$ compute, constant state at inference, quality competitive with attention at moderate scale. Chapter 38 built the encrypted price list: multiplications cost depth, non-polynomials cost far more, and softmax is the worst offender in the block. Put them together and a question falls out that neither chapter asks. Under encryption, is a selective SSM block cheaper than an attention block?

The answer turns on one structural fact. Attention's depth is independent of $T$ — the score matrix costs one ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication however many tokens it holds — but it pays for softmax on the critical path. A selective SSM has no softmax at all, but its recurrence is sequential in $T$, and a sequential recurrence of length $T$ has depth $T$, which is fatal. The escape is the one Chapter 21 used to make Mamba trainable: the recurrence is linear in the state, so step composition is associative, so a parallel scan replaces the sequential chain.

Theorem 42.1 (Depth of an encrypted selective scan)

Let the discretized recurrence be $h_t = \bar{A}_t h_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t x_t$ with $\bar A_t$ diagonal, and write $b_t = \bar B_t x_t$. Suppose $\bar A_t$ and $b_t$ are available as ciphertexts at level $\ell_0$, laid out across slots along the sequence axis. Then all $h_t$, $0 \le t < T$, can be computed at multiplicative depth exactly $\lceil \log_2 T\rceil$ below $\ell_0$, using $\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ rotations in each of the two lanes — $2\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ in total — and no non-polynomial operation.

Proof. The affine maps $h \mapsto A h + b$ form a monoid under composition, with $(A_1,b_1)\star(A_2,b_2) = (A_2A_1,\;A_2b_1+b_2)$ — $\star$ meaning “apply the first, then the second” — and identity $(1,0)$; associativity is associativity of function composition. The Kogge–Stone inclusive scan performs $\lceil \log_2 T\rceil$ rounds, round $j$ combining each slot with the slot $2^j$ positions earlier, after which every slot holds the composition over a window of length $2^{j+1}$; after $\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ rounds every prefix is complete. Each round is one slot rotation per lane ($A$ and $b$) followed by one ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication in each lane, and the two lanes are evaluated in parallel — so one level per round, $\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ in total. Rotations cost zero levels by Chapter 38's convention. $\blacksquare$

Two remarks, both bearing on whether the theorem is usable. First, the scan algorithm choice inverts under encryption. Blelloch's work-efficient scan performs $O(T)$ operations across an up-sweep and down-sweep, $2\lceil\log_2 T\rceil - 1$ rounds; Kogge–Stone performs $O(T\log T)$ operations in $\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ rounds. On a GPU, where work is the constraint, Blelloch wins. Under CKKS the extra work happens inside slots of the same ciphertext and is free, while every extra round is a level off the forty Chapter 38 allowed itself. Minimizing depth beats minimizing work, decisively. Certain

Second, the rotation is cyclic, so round $j$ wraps the last $2^j$ slots to the front and corrupts them. The standard repair is a plaintext 0/1 mask — and a mask is a multiplication, costing a level, doubling scan depth to $2\lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ and, as the artifact prints, destroying the result. The depth-free repair is to pad the slot array to $2T$ with the monoid identity $(1,0)$, so the cyclic wrap brings in identities. The price is $2\times$ the slot footprint, paid in rotations and memory rather than levels.

Common Pitfall

Charging the scan a masking multiplication per round. With $\texttt{scan\_mask\_levels}=1$ the artifact reports the selective block at depth 51 at $T=2048$ against attention's 44, and the crossover collapses from $T=32{,}768$ to $T=128$ — the architecture's entire advantage, deleted by one implementation detail that a plaintext programmer would never think about. Identity padding, not masking, is what makes Theorem 42.1 true in practice.

With the scan priced, the block comparison is mechanical. Artifact 42.1 rebuilds Chapter 38's ledger as a directed acyclic graph, charging parallel branches $\max(\text{parents}) + \text{cost}$ rather than summing — necessary because both blocks have parallel branches (Q/K/V; the SSM gate path) that a linear ledger misprices.

Example 42.1 (Block depth at $d=768$, twelve heads)

A Mamba-style block replaces attention and the MLP, so the honest comparison is one SSM block against attention-plus-MLP. Walking the SSM critical path at four Goldschmidt iterations and degree-15 gating polynomials: RMSNorm $1 + 8 + 1 = 10$; input projection $11$; depthwise causal convolution (plaintext kernel) $12$; SiLU $+4 = 16$; the $\Delta$ projection $17$; softplus $+4 = 21$; $\Delta \odot \mathrm{diag}(A)$ $22$; the discretizing exponential $+4 = 26$; the scan $26 + \lceil\log_2 T\rceil$; the $C$ contraction $27 + \lceil\log_2 T\rceil$; the output gate $28 + \lceil\log_2 T\rceil$; the output projection $29 + \lceil\log_2 T\rceil$. So the closed form is $29 + \lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ against attention's flat $44$. At $T = 128$: $36$ versus $44$. At $T = 8192$: $42$ versus $44$. The crossover is $\lceil\log_2 T\rceil = 15$, i.e. $T = 32{,}768$ — the SSM block is shallower at every context length anyone currently serves under encryption. Of attention's 28 critical-path levels, 13 are softmax (exp 4, Goldschmidt reciprocal 8, normalize 1); the SSM block spends zero.

The rotation side is more lopsided still: attention's ciphertext-ciphertext products need rotate-and-sum reductions scaling with $H \cdot T$, while the scan's rotations scale with the activation-ciphertext count times $\log_2 T$. The artifact reports 41,880 rotations per attention block against 528 per SSM block at $T=128$, and 3,859,968 against 36,096 at $T=8192$ — roughly $79\times$ and $107\times$. These are counts under one packing convention; a better attention packing would narrow them, so the direction is the claim, not the factor. Likely

State the conclusion with its dependence intact. The crossover moves with the softmax budget: at a degree-3 exponential and two Goldschmidt iterations — the aggressive end of Chapter 39's frontier — the artifact reports attention at depth 38 and a crossover at $T=512$; at degree 63 and six iterations, depth 50 and a crossover past $10^6$. Contested This book's position: on current practice (degree 15–31, three to five Goldschmidt iterations, because lower degrees have not been shown to hold accuracy generatively), the SSM block is shallower at every practical $T$, and the eight-level gap at $T{=}128$ is two-thirds of a bootstrap per layer at Chapter 38's twelve levels reclaimed per refresh. A demonstrated degree-3-class softmax holding perplexity on a generative benchmark would overturn it.

42.3.1 What has actually been published

The claim "nobody has built it" needs narrowing to survive contact with the literature, so here is the honest status as of writing. Verify: this is the fastest-moving corner of the area; re-run the search before writing an introduction.

Encrypted state-space inference does exist. Brito's Public-Decay Homomorphic State Space Models for Private Sequence Inference (arXiv 2605.16647) is what its title says: a state-space block under FHE whose carried state is updated by ciphertext-plaintext decay, pushing ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication onto a local write path. The paper reports encrypted classifications matching plaintext exactly on full Rotten Tomatoes and SST-2 validation splits, at 0.7505 and 0.7420 accuracy over frozen fastText features, and roughly $5\times$ the speed of HE-friendly polynomial attention on the same workloads. The decisive word is public: the decay is input-independent, so this is an S4-class time-invariant system, not Chapter 21's selective one. Its framing is candid about why — input-dependent encrypted gates multiplying the encrypted carried state make the carry path consume depth through time, the problem Theorem 42.1 solves and that paper sidesteps.

Selective Mamba exists under a different threat model: MPCMamba (Journal of Information Security and Applications, vol. 93, 2025) runs Mamba inference under secure multi-party computation, reporting accuracy and latency improvements over MPCViT on CIFAR and Tiny-ImageNet. Under MPC there is no fixed depth budget — depth costs communication rounds, not a bootstrap — so this section's calculus does not apply — which is why the FHE side is still open.

So the contribution-shaped hole is narrower and more defensible than "encrypted SSMs": a selective, input-dependent state-space block evaluated non-interactively under CKKS at language-model scale, with a reported depth ledger, a bootstrapping schedule, and generative quality against a plaintext reference. Every piece is in this book — Chapter 21 the recurrence and scan, Chapter 39 softplus, SiLU and the exponential as polynomials on bounded intervals, Chapter 38 the packing and ledger, Chapter 41 the generation-time accounting. And there the SSM's second advantage appears, larger than the first: its state is $O(1)$ in $T$, so Example 41.1's 200$\times$ encrypted-KV-cache blow-up does not occur. What replaces it is a state ciphertext losing one level per token, bootstrapped on a metronome. That trade — unbounded cache growth versus per-token bootstrapping of a small state — is the most under-examined question in encrypted serving. Likely

42.5 Choosing a Problem: Three Concrete Candidates

Candidate 1: the encrypted selective-SSM block (Chapters 21, 38, 39, 41). Scope it as one layer, not one model. Deliverables: a CKKS implementation of Theorem 42.1's scan with identity padding; polynomial softplus, SiLU and $\exp$ on measured activation ranges with Section 39.7's drift control; a depth ledger and bootstrap schedule; a plaintext-versus-encrypted perplexity comparison at $T \in \{512, 2048\}$. State the risk up front: $\bar A_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$ must be accurate over the range $\Delta_t$ actually takes, and if $\Delta$ drifts outside the approximation interval the scan diverges silently. That is the experiment deciding whether the idea works — run it first, in plaintext, with a polynomial surrogate.

Candidate 2: routing-oblivious MoE under pure FHE (Chapters 22, 39, 40). MoE's efficiency comes from not computing every expert; encryption forbids the data-dependent branch that skips them, so a naive encrypted MoE evaluates all $E$ experts and is strictly worse than the dense model it replaces. CryptoMoE answers with balanced routing plus MPC subprotocols. The FHE-only question: can a soft polynomial routing weight — no top-$k$, no comparison, all experts evaluated and combined by a low-degree gate — be trained to match hard routing's quality? That converts a cryptographic problem into a Chapter 22 training problem, and the negative result is publishable too.

Candidate 3: depth-aware scheme-switch placement (Chapters 34, 39, 40). The primitives ship. The optimizer does not: given a network graph, a level budget, and per-operator error tolerances, decide for each non-polynomial operator whether to spend CKKS levels on a polynomial or switch to FHEW/TFHE and pay a functional bootstrap. This is discrete optimization over a DAG with a cost model — the shape of Chapter 34's mixed-precision assignment and of ATLAS's per-layer search from Section 40.4, solvable with the same machinery. The most tractable of the three and the least glamorous, which is usually a good sign.

Whichever you pick, apply Definition 42.1 to your own work first: report the depth ledger, the slot utilization, the plaintext reference for the modified architecture, and what your timing excludes. A paper doing those four things is worth more than one with a better headline number and none of them.

Artifact 42.1 — The Research Pitch as Arithmetic

This module extends Artifact 38.1's cost model with a selective-SSM block and prices it against attention at matched width across $T \in \{128, 512, 2048, 8192\}$, depth being a longest path through an explicit DAG. Three self-checks: memoized longest path against brute-force path enumeration, exact reproduction of Chapter 38's hand ledger, and a NumPy Kogge–Stone scan (np.roll for slot rotation) against the sequential recurrence, with multiplicative depth tracked per slot. Counting model only: no FHE library is imported and nothing is timed.

"""Artifact 42.1 -- the research pitch as arithmetic: an attention block and a
selective-SSM block, priced in CKKS depth and rotations at matched width.

COUNTING MODEL in the sense of Chapter 38: nothing is encrypted, no FHE
library is imported, every number is a longest-path count in a labelled
circuit -- not a measurement.  Two extensions of the Chapter 38 ledger:
depth becomes a longest path through an explicit DAG (so parallel branches
are charged max(parents)+cost, not a sum), and a selective-SSM block is
added -- associative scan of depth ceil(log2 T), input-dependent gating
polynomials, no softmax.  Self-checks: DAG longest path vs. brute-force
path enumeration; exact reproduction of Chapter 38's hand ledger; and a
NumPy Kogge-Stone scan (np.roll for slot rotation) checked against the
sequential recurrence with multiplicative depth tracked per slot.
Conventions (Chapter 38): pt-ct = ct-ct mult = 1 level; rotation and
addition = 0; degree-k polynomial = ceil(log2(k+1)) levels; Goldschmidt
reciprocal and rsqrt = 2 levels per iteration.
"""
import numpy as np
from math import ceil, log2, isqrt

def poly_depth(k):                 # Paterson-Stockmeyer depth, degree k
    return ceil(log2(k + 1))

GOLD = 2                           # Goldschmidt levels per iteration

# ------------------------------------------------------------- DAG machinery
# A block is a dict: name -> (levels, [parents], category).  Sources: no parents.
def dag_depth(nodes):
    memo = {}
    def d(n):
        if n not in memo:
            lv, par, _ = nodes[n]
            memo[n] = lv + (max(d(p) for p in par) if par else 0)
        return memo[n]
    return max(d(n) for n in nodes)

def dag_depth_bruteforce(nodes):
    """Every root-to-node path, enumerated. Exponential; these graphs are tiny."""
    best = 0
    def walk(n, acc):
        nonlocal best
        acc += nodes[n][0]
        best = max(best, acc)
        for p in nodes[n][1]:
            walk(p, acc)
    for n in nodes:
        walk(n, 0)
    return best

def cat_levels(nodes, cat):
    return sum(lv for lv, _, c in nodes.values() if c == cat)

# ---------------------------------------------------------- block circuits
def _norm(r):                      # LayerNorm / RMSNorm: square, rsqrt, apply
    return {"ln_var": (1, [], "norm"), "ln_rsqrt": (GOLD * r, ["ln_var"], "norm"),
            "ln_apply": (1, ["ln_rsqrt"], "norm")}

def attention_block(cfg):
    r, kx, ri = cfg["norm_iters"], cfg["exp_deg"], cfg["recip_iters"]
    return dict(_norm(r), **{
        "Wq":       (1, ["ln_apply"], "matmul"),
        "Wk":       (1, ["ln_apply"], "matmul"),
        "Wv":       (1, ["ln_apply"], "matmul"),
        "scale":    (1, ["Wq"], "matmul"),              # 1/sqrt(d_k)
        "QKt":      (1, ["scale", "Wk"], "matmul"),     # ct-ct: acts x acts
        "sm_exp":   (poly_depth(kx),  ["QKt"],    "softmax"),
        "sm_recip": (GOLD * ri,       ["sm_exp"], "softmax"),
        "sm_norm":  (1, ["sm_recip", "sm_exp"],   "softmax"),
        "AV":       (1, ["sm_norm", "Wv"], "matmul"),
        "Wo":       (1, ["AV"], "matmul")})

def mlp_block(cfg):
    return dict(_norm(cfg["norm_iters"]), **{
        "W_up":   (1, ["ln_apply"], "matmul"),
        "gelu":   (poly_depth(cfg["gelu_deg"]), ["W_up"], "activation"),
        "W_down": (1, ["gelu"], "matmul")})

def ssm_block(cfg, T):
    """Selective (Mamba-style) block; one such block replaces attention AND MLP.
    RMSNorm -> in_proj -> depthwise causal conv -> SiLU -> selective Delta,B,C
    -> discretise -> parallel scan -> output gate -> out_proj."""
    kg = cfg["ssm_gate_deg"]
    scan = ceil(log2(T)) * (1 + cfg["scan_mask_levels"])
    return dict(_norm(cfg["norm_iters"]), **{
        "in_proj_x": (1, ["ln_apply"], "matmul"),
        "in_proj_z": (1, ["ln_apply"], "matmul"),       # gate branch, parallel
        "conv1d":    (1, ["in_proj_x"], "matmul"),      # plaintext depthwise kernel
        "silu_x":    (poly_depth(kg), ["conv1d"], "activation"),
        "silu_z":    (poly_depth(kg), ["in_proj_z"], "activation"),
        "dt_proj":   (1, ["silu_x"], "matmul"),
        "softplus":  (poly_depth(cfg["softplus_deg"]), ["dt_proj"], "activation"),
        "dt_A":      (1, ["softplus"], "scan"),         # Delta * diag(A), pt-ct
        "Abar_exp":  (poly_depth(cfg["ssm_exp_deg"]), ["dt_A"], "activation"),
        "B_proj":    (1, ["silu_x"], "matmul"),
        "C_proj":    (1, ["silu_x"], "matmul"),
        "Bbar":      (1, ["softplus", "B_proj"], "scan"),   # Delta (.) B
        "Bx":        (1, ["Bbar", "silu_x"], "scan"),       # (.) x_t
        "scan":      (scan, ["Abar_exp", "Bx"], "scan"),    # Kogge-Stone
        "y_C":       (1, ["scan", "C_proj"], "scan"),
        "gate":      (1, ["y_C", "silu_z"], "activation"),
        "out_proj":  (1, ["gate"], "matmul")})

# ----------------------------------------------------- rotations (Chapter 38)
def rot_bsgs(d):
    n1 = isqrt(d)
    while d % n1:
        n1 -= 1
    return n1 + d // n1 - 2

def rotations(cfg, T, kind):
    d, H, s = cfg["d"], cfg["heads"], cfg["slots"]
    n_x = ceil(T * d / s)
    if kind == "attention":                       # 12 dxd matmul equivalents
        w, cc = 12 * n_x * rot_bsgs(d), 2 * H * T * (ceil(log2(T)) +
                                                     ceil(log2(d // H)))
    else:                                         # in_proj x2 + out_proj
        w, cc = 3 * n_x * rot_bsgs(d), 2 * n_x * ceil(log2(T))
    return w + cc, w, cc

# ------------------------------------- simulate the scan, and its depth, in NumPy
def kogge_stone_scan(A, b):
    """Inclusive scan of the affine monoid (A1,b1)o(A2,b2) = (A2 A1, A2 b1 + b2),
    np.roll standing in for slot rotation.  Slots [T,2T) hold the monoid identity
    (1,0), so the cyclic wrap brings in identities and no masking multiply is
    needed (cost: 2x the slot footprint).  Returns (h, rotations, depth)."""
    T = len(A)
    Ap = np.concatenate([A, np.ones(T)])
    bp = np.concatenate([b, np.zeros(T)])
    dep, rots = np.zeros(2 * T), 0
    for j in range(ceil(log2(T))):
        sh = 1 << j
        Ash, bsh = np.roll(Ap, sh), np.roll(bp, sh)
        rots += 2                                        # one rotation per lane
        dep = np.maximum(dep, np.roll(dep, sh)) + 1      # one ct-ct mult / step
        Ap, bp = Ap * Ash, Ap * bsh + bp
    return bp[:T], rots, int(dep[:T].max())

def sequential_scan(A, b):
    h, out = 0.0, []
    for t in range(len(A)):
        h = A[t] * h + b[t]
        out.append(h)
    return np.array(out)

# --------------------------------------------------------------------- pricing
BASE = dict(d=768, heads=12, slots=2 ** 15, norm_iters=4, recip_iters=4,
            exp_deg=15, gelu_deg=15, ssm_gate_deg=15, softplus_deg=15,
            ssm_exp_deg=15, scan_mask_levels=0)

def attn_depth(cfg):
    return dag_depth(attention_block(cfg)) + dag_depth(mlp_block(cfg))

def ssm_depth(cfg, T):
    return dag_depth(ssm_block(cfg, T))

def crossover(cfg):
    """Largest power-of-two T at which the SSM block is still no deeper."""
    k = 1 + cfg["scan_mask_levels"]
    const = ssm_depth(cfg, 2) - k                   # depth = const + k*ceil(log2 T)
    return 2 ** ((attn_depth(cfg) - const) // k), const

42.6 Closing: The Two Books, Joined

Parts I through VII are a book about a price list where matrix multiplication dominates, memory bandwidth is the wall, and softmax is a rounding error. Part VIII is a book about a price list where multiplication is metered by an irreplaceable depth budget, memory movement is rotations, and softmax is half the block. The same models appear in both, and almost every design conclusion inverts.

That inversion is the opportunity. An architecture chosen for GPU efficiency is not thereby a good encrypted architecture — and Chapter 21's sub-quadratic line, built to escape attention's quadratic cost in plaintext, turns out by the arithmetic of Section 42.3 to escape something more valuable under encryption: the softmax that eats 13 of 28 levels, and the cache that grows without bound. Nobody set out to design an FHE-friendly architecture; the field designed one by accident, chasing a different constraint, and then did not check.

Checking is what this book has tried to teach. Every chapter ended in a runnable artifact that asserts its own claims, because the alternative — believing a number because a paper printed it — is how Section 42.2's overclaims propagate. The frontier is not a place where you need permission to work. It is a place where two chapters touch and the arithmetic has not been done. Do the arithmetic. Publish the ledger.

Key Takeaways
  • Read every paper in this literature through the nine-point extraction of Definition 42.1; the two points machine-learning readers skip — the depth ledger and slot utilization — are the two that determine whether the reported latency means anything. Certain
  • CKKS's approximate decryption breaks plain IND-CPA (Li–Micciancio, EUROCRYPT 2021); noise flooding is the fix, it costs precision, precision costs polynomial degree, and degree costs depth — so a system silent about IND-CPA$^{\mathrm{D}}$ is benchmarking a cheaper scheme than it claims. Certain
  • A selective SSM block has depth $29 + \lceil\log_2 T\rceil$ against an attention-plus-MLP block's flat $44$ under matched assumptions, with zero softmax levels and roughly $10^2\times$ fewer rotations in the counting model — and the crossover sits at $T = 32{,}768$, beyond any context length currently served under encryption. Likely
  • Which block wins depends on $T$ and on the achievable softmax degree: a degree-3 exponential moves the crossover down to $T = 512$. The comparison is computable, and the point is that it is computable, not that one side is destined to win. Contested
  • Published encrypted state-space work uses public, input-independent decay, and selective Mamba has been done only under MPC; the open contribution is narrower than "encrypted SSMs" and correspondingly more defensible — a selective block under non-interactive CKKS, with a ledger and generative quality reported.
