42.1 How to Read This Literature

The encrypted-inference literature is small enough to read exhaustively — a few dozen load-bearing papers — but it punishes casual reading, because the headline numbers are generated under wildly different conventions. Chapter 40 showed the failure concretely: latency figures across systems differ in threat model, sequence length, hardware, and what is excluded from the clock, so they are not comparable, and any table that juxtaposes them without saying so is an error. The defense is a fixed extraction template. Fill in all nine fields before you let a paper update your beliefs; a field you cannot fill from the paper is itself a finding.

42.2 What Is Routinely Overclaimed

Five claims recur. Each is stated here with the experiment that would settle it — and each settling experiment is, deliberately, a publishable contribution.

One forward pass, sold as inference. "GPT-2 under FHE in $X$ minutes" almost always times one prefill. Chapter 41 showed why generation is a different problem: an encrypted KV cache, per-step repacking, and bootstrap placement across hundreds of sequential decode steps. Settling experiment: report per-token latency for a 64-token generation, encrypted cache included, decode step timed separately from prefill. Recent preprints have begun exactly this — Cachemir (arXiv 2602.11470) and CryptoGen (arXiv 2602.08798) both target encrypted KV-cache reuse — which is why the claim is now overclaimed rather than unexamined. Verify

Fixed-degree accuracy. "Our softmax replacement preserves accuracy" is validated at $T \le 128$ and quietly assumed to hold at $T = 8192$. Chapter 39 explained why it will not: the softmax denominator is a sum of $T$ exponentials, its dynamic range grows with $T$, and a polynomial fitted on a fixed interval degrades as its inputs leave that interval. Settling experiment: hold every degree fixed, sweep $T$, plot accuracy against $T$. To my knowledge no paper publishes this curve for a transformer under CKKS. Likely

Cross-system speedup ratios. "16× faster than [prior system]" where the two numbers share no model, hardware, threat model, or timing boundary. Chapter 40 dissected this; the settling experiment is a single-harness reimplementation under one threat model — tedious, unglamorous, and probably the highest-value service paper available in this field. Certain

Headline security. "128-bit secure" from a lattice estimator, while the protocol shares decryptions of approximate CKKS results. Li and Micciancio (EUROCRYPT 2021) showed that CKKS's approximation noise leaks secret-key information when decryptions are revealed; the fix — noise flooding — costs precision and must be parameterized and reported. Settling experiment: state the flooding parameters and the resulting effective precision, or state explicitly that decryptions are never shared. Certain

Counting models presented as measurements. This book's own genre. A depth-and-rotation model like Chapter 38's (and this chapter's artifact) predicts ordering, not wall clock: it weighs every level equally, ignores memory traffic, and ignores the fact that a rotation's cost depends on the level it happens at. Settling experiment: calibrate the counting model against per-operation microbenchmarks from a real library and report where the ordering it predicts survives contact. That is candidate B in Section 42.5.

Common Pitfall

Computing a block's multiplicative depth by summing the ledger instead of taking the critical path. Depth is the longest chain of ciphertext multiplications; parallel branches meet at $\max(\cdot)+1$, because CKKS lets you drop the fresher operand to the deeper operand's level for free. The first version of this chapter's artifact summed the SSM ledger and got $23 + \lceil \log_2 T \rceil$; the gate branch (SiLU on $z$) runs in parallel with the scan branch, and the true critical path is $19 + \lceil \log_2 T \rceil$. Four phantom levels is the difference between fitting a leveled parameter set and paying for a bootstrap. The same bug in reverse — treating serial softmax stages as parallel — flatters attention. Tag every value with its depth and propagate $\max$, as the artifact's scan simulation does; never add up a list by eye.

42.3 The SSM Opportunity: Depth Arithmetic

The CKKS facts this section uses, stated once (Chapter 38 derived them; a dedicated FHE text covers the cryptography): a ciphertext holds a vector of slots; plaintext–ciphertext and ciphertext–ciphertext multiplications each consume one level of a fixed budget after rescaling; rotations and additions consume none; a degree-$k$ polynomial costs $\lceil \log_2(k+1) \rceil$ levels via Paterson–Stockmeyer; and when the budget runs out, bootstrapping refreshes it at a cost of hundreds of milliseconds to seconds per ciphertext plus ~10–20 levels of overhead. Depth is the scarce resource; parallel work is nearly free.

Now recall Chapter 21. A selective SSM (Mamba-style) processes a sequence through the recurrence $h_t = \bar A_t \odot h_{t-1} + \bar B_t \odot x_t$, where the decay $\bar A_t$ and input gate $\bar B_t$ are functions of the input — that is the selectivity — and the output is $y_t = C_t^\top h_t$, gated by $\mathrm{SiLU}(z_t)$. In the clear, its virtues are linear time and constant state. Under encryption, it has three virtues that matter more. First, there is no softmax: no $\exp$ over a $T \times T$ score matrix, no encrypted reciprocal of a sum whose range grows with $T$. The non-polynomial inventory shrinks to elementwise gates on inputs whose range is fixed by the preceding norm — precisely the case where Chapter 39's fixed-interval approximations are honest. Second, there is no $T \times T$ object at all: the largest tensor is the state sequence, $O(T \cdot d \cdot n_{\text{state}})$ versus attention's $O(H T^2)$ scores. Third, the recurrence is an associative scan, and associative scans have logarithmic depth.

Definition 42.1 (Critical-path depth)

For a circuit of ciphertext operations, the depth of a value is the length of the longest chain of level-consuming multiplications from any input to that value; the depth of a block is the maximum over its outputs. Parallel branches do not add: if $u$ and $v$ have depths $d_u, d_v$, then $u \odot v$ has depth $\max(d_u, d_v) + 1$.

Theorem 42.1 (Scan depth)

Let $f_t(h) = a_t h + b_t$ with $a_t, b_t$ ciphertexts of depth $0$, and let $h_t = (f_t \circ \cdots \circ f_1)(h_0)$. The Hillis–Steele scan computes all of $h_1, \dots, h_T$ in exactly $\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ levels, and no algorithm computes $h_T$ in fewer.

Proof. Represent $f_t$ by the pair $(a_t, b_t)$. Composition is $(a_2, b_2) \circ (a_1, b_1) = (a_2 a_1,\; a_2 b_1 + b_2)$, which is associative: composing three pairs in either order yields $(a_3 a_2 a_1,\; a_3 a_2 b_1 + a_3 b_2 + b_3)$. One composition costs two multiplications that can be executed in parallel, hence one level, with the new depth $\max$ of the operands' depths plus one. The scan performs stages $s = 1, 2, 4, \dots$: at stage $s$, every position $t \ge s$ composes with position $t - s$. By induction, after stage $2^j$ each position holds the composite of at most $2^{j+1}$ consecutive $f$'s at depth at most $j+1$, so $\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ stages produce every prefix composite $h_t = A_t h_0 + B_t$. For the lower bound: $h_T$ contains the monomial $a_T a_{T-1} \cdots a_1 b_0'$-free term $\prod$-structure — concretely, $A_T = \prod_{t=1}^{T} a_t$ is a product of $T$ depth-$0$ ciphertexts, and any multiplication tree over $T$ leaves has depth at least $\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$. $\blacksquare$

Example 42.1 (A four-step scan by hand)

Take $T=4$, $a = (0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5)$, $b = (1,1,1,1)$, $h_0 = 0$. Sequentially: $h_1 = 1$, $h_2 = 1.5$, $h_3 = 1.75$, $h_4 = 1.875$. Scan, stage $s=1$ (positions $2..4$ compose with their left neighbor): $A = (0.5, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25)$, $B = (1, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5)$, depth tags $(0,1,1,1)$. Stage $s=2$ (positions $3, 4$ compose with positions $1, 2$): $A = (0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625)$, $B = (1,\; 1.5,\; 0.25 \cdot 1 + 1.5 = 1.75,\; 0.25 \cdot 1.5 + 1.5 = 1.875)$, depth tags $(0, 1, 2, 2)$. Two stages, matching $\lceil \log_2 4 \rceil = 2$, and $B$ equals the sequential $h$ exactly.

42.3.1 The two ledgers

Chapter 38's attention sub-block ledger, at degree-15 polynomials and 4 Goldschmidt iterations: norm ($1 + 8 + 1$), QKV projection ($1$), scale ($1$), $QK^\top$ ($1$), softmax $\exp$ ($4$), softmax reciprocal ($8$), normalize ($1$), attention-times-$V$ ($1$), output projection ($1$) — $28$ levels, independent of $T$, because the $T$-indexed work is all rotations and additions. The selective-SSM block at the same conventions: norm ($10$), input projection ($1$), depthwise convolution ($1$), gating polynomial for the decay and input gate ($4$), scan ($\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$), readout $C \odot h$ ($1$); in parallel, the SiLU gate branch reaches depth $15$; the branches merge at $\max(\cdot) + 1$, then the output projection adds one more. Critical path: $19 + \lceil \log_2 T \rceil$. The softmax-related line items — $\exp$, reciprocal, normalize, $13$ levels in the attention ledger — are zero by construction: there is no softmax to approximate.

So neither block dominates. At these conventions the SSM wins depth strictly for $T \le 256$, ties through $T = 512$ ($28 = 28$), and loses from $T = 513$ up: $\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ eventually exceeds a constant. But the constant is not constant. The attention figure holds the softmax approximation fixed while $T$ grows, and Section 42.2's second overclaim says exactly why that flatters it: raise the $\exp$ to degree 63 and the reciprocal to 6 iterations — a defensible budget for long sequences — and attention costs $34$ levels, which the SSM beats all the way to $T = 32768$. Meanwhile the SSM's $\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ levels buy exact multiplications: the scan is arithmetic, not approximation, so its depth cost carries no accuracy cost, only CKKS's precision drift (Chapter 41), which is a calibration problem, not a degree problem. Likely And depth is only one axis: the artifact's counting model puts attention at $3{,}777{,}024$ rotations and $\sim 3.39$M ciphertext multiplications per block at $T = 8192$ against the SSM's $132{,}864$ and $\sim 0.18$M — an $18.9\times$ multiplication gap that grows with $T$ because attention materializes $24{,}576$ score ciphertexts where the SSM's state sequence needs $6{,}144$.

Has anyone built it? Almost, and honestly: not yet. Brito's "Public-Decay Homomorphic State Space Models for Private Sequence Inference" (arXiv 2605.16647, 2026) is, as far as I can determine, the first published encrypted-SSM inference result — and its central design choice concedes exactly the hard part. The decay is public: the state transition is a plaintext–ciphertext multiplication, keeping the recurrence cheap, and the model is evaluated sequentially over frozen fastText features on sentence classification. The paper reports 0.7505 and 0.7420 accuracy on Rotten Tomatoes and SST-2 with the encrypted path matching plaintext classifications exactly, and roughly $5\times$ lower latency than an HE-friendly polynomial-attention baseline on the same workload. Verify That is a real data point in favor of the architecture-fit thesis — and it leaves the actual prize untouched: selective transitions, where $\bar A_t$ is itself a ciphertext, the scan's products are ciphertext–ciphertext, the depth analysis above becomes load-bearing, and the model class is the one that Chapter 21 showed matches transformers. Nobody has published that, at any scale, as of this writing. Verify

42.5 Choosing a Problem: Three Concrete Candidates

Candidate A — build the encrypted selective-SSM block. One Mamba-style block under RNS-CKKS in OpenFHE: RMSNorm via Goldschmidt, gate polynomials from Chapter 39's toolkit, the Hillis–Steele scan over packed ciphertexts, benchmarked at matched width against a polynomial attention block (Zimerman et al., ICML 2024, is the natural baseline recipe). The first milestone is deliberately small: block-level latency and precision at $T = 1024$, with the depth ledger measured, not asserted. The risk is also concrete: the scan's ciphertext–ciphertext products chain $\lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ rescalings, and precision drift across them (Chapter 41) is the experiment's real question — if effective precision collapses at $T = 8192$, that negative result is itself the paper. Equipped by Chapters 17, 21, 38, 39.

Candidate B — calibrate the crossover. Turn the counting model of Chapter 38 and this chapter into a measured cost atlas: per-operation microbenchmarks (multiply, rotate, rescale, bootstrap, at each level) from one library on one machine, then a validated predictor of block cost over $(T, d, \text{degrees}, \text{packing})$. This is the field's missing methods paper; it converts every cross-system comparison complaint in Section 42.2 into a tool, exactly as scaling-law methodology (Chapter 24) converted architecture debates into fits. Equipped by Chapters 24, 30, 38, 40, 41.

Candidate C — encrypted PEFT on an HE-friendly backbone. If candidate A works, LoRA on top of it: the backward pass of the SSM block is itself a scan (Chapter 17's BPTT, Chapter 11's reverse mode), so gradient computation inherits the same log-depth structure — adapters $\Delta W = BA$ with rank $r \ll d$ keep the optimizer state small enough to bootstrap sanely. The existing encrypted-LoRA line (Section 42.4) is interactive or federated; the non-interactive version on a backbone designed for depth is unclaimed. 42.6 Closing: The Two Books, Joined

There are two books inside this one. Parts I through VII are a book about learned functions: what they can represent, how they are trained, what they cost. Part VIII is a book about a compiler target — an arithmetic in which multiplication is metered, comparison is a research problem, and a forward pass is a circuit with a depth budget. Neither book is novel on its own; both have excellent dedicated treatments. The contribution surface is their join, and the join is computable: this chapter's final artifact is nothing but Chapter 38's cost model meeting Chapter 21's architecture, and it yields a concrete, falsifiable research pitch in two hundred lines of arithmetic. That is the general method being demonstrated, and it is the last thing this book has to teach. Take an object from the first book — a scan, a router, an adapter, a decoding loop. Price it in the second book's currency. Where the price is surprising and the literature is silent, you have found the frontier — and you are, at that moment, better equipped for it than almost anyone, because you have both books in your head at once.

Artifact 42.1 — The Research Pitch as Arithmetic

This artifact extends Chapter 38's CKKS counting model — a counting model, not an FHE implementation; nothing is encrypted — with a selective-SSM block and compares it against the attention sub-block at matched width across $T \in \{128, 512, 2048, 8192\}$ under two softmax budgets. It verifies the Hillis–Steele scan against the sequential recurrence in NumPy, tracks a per-element depth tag to confirm Theorem 42.1, reproduces Chapter 38's 28-level attention figure, asserts the $19 + \lceil \log_2 T \rceil$ SSM depth law and the zero-softmax-levels property, computes the crossover interval under both scenarios, and prints the three candidate problems with their chapters.

"""Artifact 42.1 -- The research pitch as arithmetic.

Extends the Chapter 38 CKKS COUNTING MODEL (no ciphertext is ever created)
with a selective-SSM block and compares it against the attention sub-block
at matched width across T in {128, 512, 2048, 8192}.

Conventions inherited from Artifact 38.1: pt-ct mult = 1 level, ct-ct mult
= 1 level, rotations/additions = 0 levels; a degree-k polynomial via
Paterson-Stockmeyer costs ceil(log2(k+1)) levels; Goldschmidt reciprocal
and rsqrt cost 2 levels per iteration.  Depth here is the CRITICAL PATH
(parallel branches do not add), which is what CKKS actually charges.

Self-checks: (1) a NumPy Hillis-Steele scan for h_t = a_t h_{t-1} + b_t is
verified against the sequential recurrence, and a per-element depth tag is
tracked through the scan to confirm max depth == ceil(log2 T); (2) the
attention ledger reproduces the 28-level figure of Artifact 38.1; (3) the
crossover interval is computed and asserted, not eyeballed.
"""
import numpy as np
from math import ceil, log2, isqrt

# ------------------------------------------------------------- depth atoms
def poly_depth(k):   # Paterson-Stockmeyer depth of a degree-k polynomial
    return ceil(log2(k + 1))

def poly_mults(k):   # PS non-scalar mult count ~ sqrt(2k) + log2 k
    return ceil((2 * k) ** 0.5) + max(0, ceil(log2(k + 1)) - 1)

RSQ_D, RSQ_M = 2, 3          # Goldschmidt rsqrt: 2 levels, 3 mults / iter
REC_D, REC_M = 2, 2          # Goldschmidt reciprocal: 2 levels, 2 mults / iter

def rot_bsgs(d):             # BSGS rotations for one d x d pt-ct matvec
    n1 = isqrt(d)
    while d % n1:
        n1 -= 1
    return n1 + d // n1 - 2

# ---------------------------------------------------- attention sub-block
def attn_ledger(kx, r_ln, r_sm):
    """(label, levels) chain; the attention path is a single serial chain."""
    return [("norm variance (ct-ct square)",             1),
            ("norm rsqrt, %d Goldschmidt iters" % r_ln,  RSQ_D * r_ln),
            ("norm apply (ct-ct)",                       1),
            ("Q,K,V projections (pt-ct)",                1),
            ("scale by 1/sqrt(d_k) (pt)",                1),
            ("Q K^T (ct-ct matmul)",                     1),
            ("softmax exp poly deg %d" % kx,             poly_depth(kx)),
            ("softmax reciprocal, %d iters" % r_sm,      REC_D * r_sm),
            ("softmax normalize (ct-ct)",                1),
            ("attention @ V (ct-ct matmul)",             1),
            ("output projection W_O (pt-ct)",            1)]

def attn_depth(kx, r_ln, r_sm):
    return sum(v for _, v in attn_ledger(kx, r_ln, r_sm))

def softmax_levels(ledger):
    return sum(v for name, v in ledger if name.startswith("softmax"))

# ---------------------------------------------------- selective-SSM block
def ssm_ledger(T, kg, r_ln):
    """Two parallel branches after the shared trunk; depth = critical path.
    State path:  norm -> in_proj -> conv -> gating poly (decay a_t and
                 input gate, deg kg) -> scan (ceil(log2 T)) -> C (.) h.
    Gate path:   norm -> in_proj -> SiLU poly (deg kg) on z.
    Then y (.) gate (1 ct-ct) and out_proj (1 pt-ct).  NO softmax lines
    exist by construction: no exp over scores, no reciprocal, no normalize."""
    trunk = [("norm variance (ct-ct square)",            1),
             ("norm rsqrt, %d Goldschmidt iters" % r_ln, RSQ_D * r_ln),
             ("norm apply (ct-ct)",                      1),
             ("in_proj to (x, z) (pt-ct)",               1)]
    state = [("depthwise conv1d (pt-ct)",                1),
             ("gating poly (decay+input), deg %d" % kg,  poly_depth(kg)),
             ("parallel scan, ceil(log2 T) stages",      ceil(log2(T))),
             ("readout C (.) h (ct-ct)",                 1)]
    gate = [("SiLU poly on z, deg %d" % kg,              poly_depth(kg))]
    tail = [("gated product y (.) SiLU(z) (ct-ct)",      1),
            ("out_proj (pt-ct)",                         1)]
    return trunk, state, gate, tail

def ssm_depth(T, kg, r_ln):
    trunk, state, gate, tail = ssm_ledger(T, kg, r_ln)
    s = lambda part: sum(v for _, v in part)
    return s(trunk) + max(s(state), s(gate)) + s(tail)

# ----------------------------------- operation counts (rotations & mults)
def attn_ops(T, d, H, kx, r_ln, r_sm, slots):
    dk = d // H
    n_x = ceil(T * d / slots)               # activation ciphertexts
    n_s = ceil(H * T * T / slots)           # T x T score ciphertexts (!)
    n_kv = ceil(T * dk / slots)             # one head's K (or V)
    rot = 4 * n_x * rot_bsgs(d) + 2 * H * T * (ceil(log2(T)) + ceil(log2(dk)))
    mults = (2 * n_x + RSQ_M * r_ln                     # one norm
             + 2 * H * T * n_kv                         # QK^T and attn@V
             + poly_mults(kx) * n_s                     # exp on every score ct
             + REC_M * r_sm + n_s)                      # reciprocal + normalize
    return rot, mults, n_s

def ssm_ops(T, d, kg, r_ln, slots, d_state=16, conv_k=4):
    d_in = 2 * d                            # Mamba-style expansion factor 2
    n_x = ceil(T * d / slots)
    n_g = ceil(T * d_in / slots)            # gate/channel sequences
    n_h = ceil(T * d_in * d_state / slots)  # the scanned state sequence
    S = ceil(log2(T))
    rot = (5 * n_x * rot_bsgs(d)            # in/out/x-dependent projections
           + (conv_k - 1) * n_g             # depthwise conv shifts
           + S * n_h)                       # one rotation per ct per stage
    mults = (2 * n_x + RSQ_M * r_ln                     # norm
             + poly_mults(kg) * (2 * n_g)               # gating + SiLU polys
             + n_h                                      # b_t = Bbar (.) x write
             + 2 * S * n_h                              # scan: 2 mults / stage
             + n_h + n_g)                               # readout + gated product
    return rot, mults, n_h

# --------------------------------------------- scan verification (NumPy)
def scan_check(T, lanes=4, rng=None):
    """Hillis-Steele scan of f_t(h) = a_t h + b_t with per-element depth tags.
    Returns (max residual vs sequential loop, number of stages, max depth)."""
    a = 0.5 + 0.5 * rng.random((T, lanes))              # decays in (0.5, 1)
    b = rng.standard_normal((T, lanes)) * 0.1
    A, B, depth, s, stages = a.copy(), b.copy(), np.zeros(T, int), 1, 0
    while s < T:
        A2, B2, d2 = A.copy(), B.copy(), depth.copy()
        A2[s:] = A[s:] * A[:-s]                         # compose F_t o F_{t-s}
        B2[s:] = A[s:] * B[:-s] + B[s:]                 # (2 mults, in parallel
        d2[s:] = np.maximum(depth[s:], depth[:-s]) + 1  #  => +1 level)
        A, B, depth, s, stages = A2, B2, d2, 2 * s, stages + 1
    h_seq, h = np.zeros(lanes), np.zeros((T, lanes))    # sequential reference
    for t in range(T):
        h_seq = a[t] * h_seq + b[t]
        h[t] = h_seq
    return np.abs(B - h).max(), stages, depth.max()     # h0 = 0 => h_t = B_t

Key Takeaways
  • Latency numbers across encrypted-inference systems are not comparable — threat models, sequence lengths, hardware, and timing boundaries all differ — so read every paper through the nine-point template and treat any unfilled field as a finding. Certain
  • Under Chapter 38's counting conventions, an attention block costs a $T$-independent 28 levels while a selective-SSM block costs $19 + \lceil \log_2 T \rceil$; which wins on depth depends on $T$ and on the softmax degree you believe, and the crossover is computable rather than a matter of taste. Likely
  • The SSM block needs zero softmax-related levels by construction and never materializes a $T \times T$ score object, so its rotation and multiplication counts beat attention's by a growing margin at long $T$ in the counting model. Likely
  • Encrypted SSM inference exists only in embryonic form — public-decay transitions on small classifiers (arXiv 2605.16647) — and the selective, ciphertext-gated, log-depth-scan version at transformer scale is unpublished as of this writing; check before you build. Verify
  • Counting models predict orderings, not wall clock: any depth-and-rotation argument, including this chapter's, must be calibrated against per-operation measurements from a real FHE library before it supports a systems claim. Certain
