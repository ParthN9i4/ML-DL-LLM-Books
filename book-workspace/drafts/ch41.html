Encrypted LLM Serving at Scale

Learning Objectives

Chapter 40 surveyed private transformer inference systems and Chapter 39 built their polynomial cores; nearly every number in those chapters describes one forward pass. This chapter asks what those systems have mostly not: what happens when you decode autoregressively, so that the KV cache of Chapter 33 becomes a growing set of ciphertexts. Three costs that a single-pass benchmark never exercises — cache memory blow-up, per-step repacking, and bootstrapping in a loop whose depth grows without bound — turn out to dominate. We derive each, verify the closed forms against a counting model in Artifact 41.1, and end with the number the field least likes to print: an honest end-to-end accounting of one generated token. Chapter 42 uses the gaps identified here as a map of where a research contribution fits.

41.1 One Forward Pass Is Not Generation

The CKKS facts this chapter consumes, stated once (a dedicated FHE text, e.g. the CKKS chapters of Lauter, Dai & Laine's Protecting Privacy through Homomorphic Encryption, covers the constructions): a ciphertext over ring degree $N$ carries $N/2$ complex slots; each ciphertext–ciphertext or masking multiplication consumes one level from a finite ladder, with a rescaling after each; slot permutations are rotations, each costing a key-switch comparable to a multiplication; and when levels run out, bootstrapping refreshes them at a cost two to three orders of magnitude above a multiplication, itself consuming part of the ladder and capping output precision. Certain

The benchmark culture of Chapter 40 is single-pass: encrypt an input, run the circuit once, decrypt one output. NEXUS (Zhang et al., NDSS 2025) reports BERT-base inference in 37.3 s on GPU with 164 MB of bandwidth in its non-interactive CKKS setting; THOR (CCS 2025) and MOAI (ePrint 2025/991) report CKKS transformer inference in the same single-pass shape, with MOAI's paper reporting an extension of its pipeline to a Llama-3-8B forward pass. These are classification- or prefill-shaped workloads: depth is fixed at compile time, every ciphertext's level schedule is known in advance, and the circuit runs once.

Generation breaks all three properties. Decoding $T$ tokens is $T$ sequential forward passes: total multiplicative depth grows linearly in $T$, so no fixed ladder survives and bootstrapping is not an occasional refresh but a metronome. The cache that makes plaintext decoding cheap — $O(td)$ attention per step instead of $O(t^2 d)$ recompute — must itself live in ciphertext, and its slot layout is frozen at encryption time. And the argmax/sampling step between passes is a comparison, the operation CKKS does worst (Chapter 39); NEXUS's secure argmax exists precisely because the output of one pass must become the input of the next without decryption.

How thin is the actual generation literature? As of this writing: PUMA (Dong et al., 2023) reports generating one token of LLaMA-7B in around five minutes — under three-party MPC, a different threat model from FHE, with per-token cost roughly flat because MPC has no depth budget. On the FHE side, the first systems to integrate a genuinely encrypted KV cache appear in 2026 preprints: Cachemir (arXiv 2602.11470) reports FHE packing algorithms designed around the cache, an "augmented bootstrapping placement" strategy, speedups of 48.83× over MOAI and 67.16× over THOR on CPU when those single-pass systems are re-run in decoding mode, and under 100 s per generated token for Llama-3-8B on GPU; CryptoGen (arXiv 2602.08798) reports a hybrid HE/secret-sharing design with persistent encrypted KV-cache reuse, near-linear per-token scaling, and 4.4–7.6× lower per-token latency than discriminative secure-inference systems adapted to decoding, evaluated on small transformers trained on WikiText-2, PTB, and LAMBADA. Verify What has not been demonstrated, in any published system we could find: multi-hundred-token generations from a modern LLM under FHE with reported output quality; interactive (sub-second) per-token latency under any encrypted threat model at LLM scale; or batch-one decode economics that close within even two orders of magnitude of plaintext. That absence is the subject of this chapter, not an aside. Likely

41.2 The Encrypted KV Cache

Plaintext first (Chapter 33): after $t$ steps, the cache holds $2Ltd$ scalars — keys and values, per layer — and at fp16 costs $4Ltd$ bytes. Under CKKS the unit of storage is the ciphertext, and the accounting changes twice: once because slots quantize storage, and again because each slot costs far more than two bytes.

Definition 41.1 (Packing density of a cached step)

Fix ring degree $N$ with $s = N/2$ slots. A packing assigns the $d$-dimensional key (or value) vector of each cached step to slots. Its density $g$ is the number of steps stored per ciphertext, $1 \le g \le \lfloor s/d \rfloor$. The compact packing takes $g = \lfloor s/d \rfloor$; the one-ciphertext-per-step packing takes $g = 1$. After $t$ steps a layer's key cache occupies $m(t) = \lceil t/g \rceil$ ciphertexts, and the full cache $2L\,m(t)$.

Memory per ciphertext is set by the RNS representation: two polynomials of $N$ coefficients over $\ell$ limbs of $w$ bytes each, i.e. $2N\ell w$ bytes at level $\ell$. The bytes-per-cached-scalar of a compact packing is therefore $2N\ell w / (gd) \approx 2N\ell w/s = 4\ell w$, independent of the model: at $\ell = 12$ limbs and $w = 8$ bytes, 384 bytes per scalar against fp16's two — a $\sim$192× expansion before any slack from $\lceil\cdot\rceil$ effects. Certain

Example 41.1 (Cache memory at $t=1024$, GPT-2-small geometry)

Take $L=12$, $d=768$, $N=2^{16}$ (so $s=32{,}768$, $g=\lfloor 32768/768\rfloor = 42$), cache held at $\ell=12$ limbs. One ciphertext: $2 \times 65536 \times 12 \times 8 = 12{,}582{,}912$ bytes $= 12.0$ MiB. At $t=1024$: $m = \lceil 1024/42 \rceil = 25$, so $2 \times 12 \times 25 = 600$ ciphertexts $= 7.03$ GB. The fp16 plaintext cache is $4 \times 12 \times 1024 \times 768 = 36$ MiB — a 200× blow-up. The $g=1$ packing needs $2 \times 12 \times 1024 = 24{,}576$ ciphertexts $= 288$ GB, 8192× fp16. Both figures are printed by Artifact 41.1.

So a single stream's encrypted cache at modest length occupies what a plaintext server spends on the weights of a 3B-parameter model. Two consequences follow. First, the batching that makes plaintext serving economical (Chapter 33) is nearly foreclosed: CKKS batching packs many independent inputs into slots, but a compact cache already spends its slots on cache steps, and every client's cache is encrypted under a different key. Second, the choice of $g$ is a genuine trade, not an optimization detail: $g=1$ makes appending free (the new step simply becomes a fresh ciphertext) but makes memory and attention cost scale with $t$ ciphertexts; compact packing divides both by $g$ but makes appending a slot-surgery problem — the subject of the next section.

41.3 Appending, Attending, Repacking

Slots are positional and immutable-in-place: to write step $t$'s key vector into slot block $(t \bmod g)$ of the current cache ciphertext, the server must (i) rotate the freshly computed key ciphertext by $(t \bmod g)\,d$ slots, (ii) multiply by a 0/1 mask plaintext to zero everything else, and (iii) add into the cache ciphertext. Step (ii) is a real multiplication: it consumes a level. Appending is never free under compact packing — per token it costs, across $K$ and $V$ and all layers, $2L$ rotations, $2L$ masking multiplications, and one level of depth on the cache lane. This is the repacking tax, and it recurs every token for the life of the stream.

41.3.1 Attending over the packed cache

With head dimension $d_k$ and heads interleaved in slots, computing scores $q \cdot k_i$ for the $g$ steps inside one cache ciphertext costs one ciphertext–ciphertext multiplication (the replicated query against the packed keys) plus $\lceil \log_2 d_k \rceil$ rotations for the rotate-and-sum inner product; the weighted-value accumulation costs one more multiplication plus $\lceil \log_2 g \rceil$ rotations per value ciphertext. Cross-ciphertext combination is slot-aligned addition, which is free in this accounting. Per token, the cache-dependent cost is therefore

$$A(t) = L\, m(t)\,\big(2\,c_{\times} + (\lceil \log_2 d_k \rceil + \lceil \log_2 g \rceil)\, c_{\mathrm{rot}}\big), \qquad m(t) = \lceil t/g \rceil,$$

which is a staircase, flat within a ciphertext and jumping by $b = L(2 + \lceil \log_2 d_k\rceil + \lceil \log_2 g \rceil)$ multiplication-equivalents every $g$ tokens (weighting rotations and ct-mults equally at $c_\times = c_{\mathrm{rot}} = 1$, key-switch-dominated ops being comparable). For the Example 41.1 geometry, $b = 12(2+6+6) = 168$; Artifact 41.1's least-squares fit of its simulated staircase recovers slope $168.000000$ with maximum residual $1.8\times 10^{-12}$. Under $g=1$ the same derivation gives per-token cost linear in $t$ with a $42\times$ steeper effective slope — the two packings trade append cost against attend cost, and neither escapes growth in $t$. The assertion the artifact enforces is the chapter's first claim in executable form: per-token cost grows with cache length under any fixed packing. Certain

Cachemir's contribution is essentially a better point on this trade surface: the paper reports an "interleaved replicated" packing for the vector–matrix products that decode-time attention produces, chosen so that the append and attend rotations share structure. Verify It does not repeal the staircase; it lowers $b$.

Common Pitfall

Level misalignment in the cache. The key written at step 10 has consumed a different number of levels than the key written at step 400 — each passed through a different number of bootstrap cycles before being cached. CKKS addition requires operands at the same level, so a naive implementation either crashes at the add-into-cache step or silently mod-switches the entire cache ciphertext down to the newest entry's level, ratcheting the whole cache toward level zero and forcing a full-cache bootstrap. The fix is a write-time contract: mod-switch every fresh $K$/$V$ to a fixed resident level $\ell_{\text{cache}}$ chosen at compile time, and budget the masking multiplication below it. Every FHE KV-cache design must state $\ell_{\text{cache}}$ somewhere; if a paper doesn't, its memory numbers are not reproducible, because $\ell$ is a factor in every byte count.

41.4 Bootstrapping Placement

Each decoded token consumes a fixed depth $d_{\text{tok}} = L\, d_{\text{layer}}$: attention scores, the softmax polynomial, the value mix, projections, LayerNorm inverse-square-root and activation polynomials, plus the append mask (§41.3). Depth per token is constant in $t$ — longer caches add rotations and multiplications, not levels — but it accrues across tokens without bound, so bootstrapping enters the loop as a fixed per-token toll rather than a rare event.

Theorem 41.1 (Greedy bootstrap count)

Model the residual stream as one serial lane consuming $D$ levels in total, starting with a fresh budget of $B$ usable levels and bootstrapping (restoring $B$) exactly when the budget is exhausted and more depth is needed. The number of bootstraps is $\lceil D/B \rceil - 1$, and no schedule that only bootstraps (never wastes levels) does better.

Proof. After $k$ bootstraps the total usable depth supplied is $(k+1)B$; the run completes when $(k+1)B \ge D$, i.e. $k \ge D/B - 1$, so the greedy count is the least integer $k$ with $k \ge \lceil D/B \rceil - 1$. Optimality: any valid schedule with $k$ bootstraps supplies at most $(k+1)B$ levels, so $k \ge D/B - 1$ for every schedule. $\blacksquare$

The serial-lane model is the honest simplification to state loudly: a real circuit has parallel lanes at different levels, and production placement (as in the FHE compilers of Chapter 40, and the "augmented" cache-aware placement Cachemir's paper reports) exploits that slack. But it changes constants, not the structure: steady-state tokens per bootstrap is $B / d_{\text{tok}}$, and for every realistic transformer this is far below one — you bootstrap many times per token, not the reverse.

Example 41.2 (Tokens per bootstrap)

With $d_{\text{layer}} = 21$ levels (1 QKV, 1 append mask, 1 scores, 6 softmax, 1 value mix, 1 output projection, $2\times 2$ LayerNorm, 1+4+1 MLP with activation) and $L = 12$: $d_{\text{tok}} = 252$. With $B = 18$ usable levels: $252/18 = 14$ bootstraps per token, i.e. $0.0714$ tokens per bootstrap. Over $T = 1024$ tokens, $D = 258{,}048$ and Theorem 41.1 gives $\lceil 258048/18\rceil - 1 = 14{,}335$; Artifact 41.1's step-by-step level simulation counts exactly $14{,}335$ (13 for the first token, whose fresh budget is free, then 14 every token thereafter).

Placement freedom that generation adds and single-pass circuits lack: the cache itself. Cache ciphertexts sit at the fixed resident level and lose one level per append-mask; refreshing them can be batched (bootstrap $2L$ cache lanes together, amortizing key-switch keys and memory traffic) and scheduled off the token's critical path, since step $t{+}1$ needs the cache only at its attention layer. Overlapping cache refresh with the MLP of the previous layer is the FHE analogue of the prefetching tricks of Chapter 33 — reported as a design element in the 2026 cache papers, not yet standard. Likely

41.5 Effective Precision: CKKS versus fp16

fp16 carries an 11-bit significand (10 stored, one implicit). A CKKS slot's effective precision is $\log_2(\Delta / \lVert e \rVert)$: scale over noise. Fresh encryptions at $\Delta = 2^{40}$ typically start near 30 bits; each level consumed costs a model-dependent fraction of a bit (rescaling rounding plus approximation error); and bootstrapping does not restore fresh precision — it caps it, with reported CKKS bootstrapping output precision in the high-teens to low-20s of bits at practical parameters. Verify The steady-state pipeline therefore lives on a sawtooth: precision $p_{\text{boot}}$ after each bootstrap, decaying by $\lambda$ bits per level for $B$ levels. The floor is $p_{\text{boot}} - \lambda B$, and it — not the fresh-encryption headline — is the number to compare against fp16. At Artifact 41.1's stated parameters ($p_{\text{boot}} = 20$, $\lambda = 0.5$, $B = 18$) the floor is exactly $11.0$ bits: fp16 parity with zero margin, and the model's implied maximum budget for fp16 parity is $B_{\max} = \lfloor (p_{\text{boot}} - 11)/\lambda \rfloor = 18$. This is a parameterized model, not a measurement — but it exposes the real coupling: raising $B$ to bootstrap less often lowers worst-case precision. Depth budget and precision budget are the same budget spent twice.

What accuracy evidence exists beyond single-pass classification? Very little. The single-pass systems of Chapter 40 report small degradations on GLUE-style classification — a task family where a half-bit of logit error rarely flips an argmax. Generation is harsher in one way and gentler in another, and the field genuinely disagrees on which wins. Contested The pessimistic position: approximation error compounds across $T$ sequential passes — each token is sampled from perturbed logits and then conditions all later steps, so quality should degrade with length, and perplexity-style evaluation under encryption over long horizons has not been published for any modern LLM. The optimistic position: greedy decoding snaps to a discrete token, which is an error-erasing nonlinearity — if the perturbation doesn't flip the argmax, the emitted prefix is exactly the plaintext one and nothing compounds. Both are right in their regimes (peaked versus flat next-token distributions). What would settle it is cheap to state and expensive to run: token-level agreement curves between encrypted and plaintext decoding versus generation length, at matched sampling settings, on a model people care about. CryptoGen's reported evaluations (small models, WikiText-2/PTB/LAMBADA) are the closest published step; no result at Llama scale exists as of this writing. Verify

41.6 GPU-Accelerated FHE

Everything above counts operations; GPUs change the constant per operation. CKKS's inner loops — NTTs and base conversions over $N=2^{16}$ rings — are memory-bandwidth-bound, which is exactly the regime GPUs win (Chapter 30). The landmark is Jung et al. (TCHES 2021), the first GPU CKKS bootstrapping: the paper reports 0.423 μs amortized bootstrapping time per bit and a 257× speedup over a single-threaded CPU baseline, achieved by memory-centric kernel fusion rather than FLOP tricks. Since then:

For the practitioner in OpenFHE, the relevant deltas are that FIDESlib and HEonGPU both position themselves against OpenFHE's CPU baseline, and that a $\sim$100 ms-class GPU bootstrap moves the bootstrap-to-multiplication ratio $R$ from the $10^2$–$10^3$ of CPU pipelines toward $10^2$ — which matters because, as §41.7 shows, $R$ is the single knob that decides whether the generation loop is bootstrap-bound. What GPU acceleration does not change: the staircase in $t$, the tokens-per-bootstrap arithmetic, and the 200× cache memory expansion — 7 GB per stream at $t=1024$ now competes with model weights for HBM. 41.7 An Honest Cost of One Token

Assemble the pieces at the Example 41.1 geometry, from Artifact 41.1's run. Decoding one token at $t=1$ costs 19,149.6 multiplication-equivalents in the counting model; at $t=1024$, 23,181.6, with the cache-attention share growing from 0.9% to 18.1%. Bootstrapping adds $14 \times R$ equivalents per token; at the [Verify]-tagged $R=100$ that is 1,400 — 6.8% of modeled time at $t=1$, diluting to 5.7% at $t=1024$. The crossover where the growing attention staircase overtakes the fixed bootstrap toll has the closed form $t^{*} = \lfloor d_{\text{tok}} R / (B\,b) \rfloor \cdot g + 1 = 337$, and the simulation's op-by-op scan finds exactly $t=337$. On a CPU pipeline with $R \sim 10^3$, the same closed form pushes $t^{*}$ past 3,000: bootstrap-bound for any realistic chat turn. That inversion is worth stating plainly: GPU bootstrapping does not remove the bootstrap problem; it relocates the bottleneck to cache traffic, which is the harder one to fix because it is bandwidth, not compute. Likely

To anchor against published wall-clock: Cachemir's paper reports under 100 s per generated token for Llama-3-8B on GPU; PUMA's paper reports about five minutes per LLaMA-7B token under three-party MPC. These two numbers come from different threat models, hardware, and timing boundaries and must not be compared to each other or to plaintext serving numbers; they jointly bound the state of the art at minutes-to-two-minutes per token for 7–8B models under cryptographic protection. Verify A plaintext A100 serves the same model at tens of milliseconds per token — three to four orders of magnitude. The honest sentence, then: encrypted generation exists as a demonstrated capability at the scale of single tokens and short sequences, and does not yet exist as a demonstrated service. The distance is measured in exactly the three quantities this chapter derived — bytes per cached scalar ($4\ell w$), the staircase slope $b$, and tokens per bootstrap ($B/d_{\text{tok}}$) — and Chapter 42 treats each as an open problem with a research surface attached.

Artifact 41.1 — An Encrypted-Generation Cost Model

This module extends Artifact 38.1's op counter to autoregressive decoding: it simulates $T=1024$ tokens with a growing KV cache under the compact packing of Definition 41.1, counting rotations, ciphertext multiplications, masking multiplications, and bootstraps under greedy level accounting. It is a counting model — no FHE library runs, and the only wall-clock-flavored input is the [Verify]-tagged ratio $R$. It asserts the chapter's three closed forms against the simulation: staircase growth with fitted slope equal to $b$, Theorem 41.1's bootstrap count, and the crossover $t^{*}$; it also evaluates the parameterized precision model of §41.5.

"""Artifact 41.1 -- Encrypted-generation cost model (extends Artifact 38.1's op counter).

COUNTING MODEL ONLY. No FHE library is invoked; nothing here is a measurement.
We count homomorphic operations (rotations, ct-ct multiplies, pt-ct multiplies,
fresh ciphertexts, bootstraps) for autoregressive decoding with a growing
encrypted KV cache under one explicitly stated packing.  Wall-clock enters only
through a single [Verify]-tagged ratio R_BOOT = bootstrap cost / ct-ct-mult
cost; effective precision is a parameterized model, not a measurement.
"""
import math
import numpy as np

# ---------------- model geometry (GPT-2-small-like; all stated, not measured) --
L, d, H, dk, dff = 12, 768, 12, 64, 3072   # layers, width, heads, head dim, FF
N = 1 << 16                                 # CKKS ring degree
SLOTS = N // 2                              # 32768 complex slots
G = SLOTS // d                              # cache steps packed per ciphertext (compact packing)
LIMB_BYTES = 8                              # one RNS limb coefficient = 8 bytes
ELL_CACHE = 12                              # limbs at which cache ciphertexts are held (stated)

# unit costs, in "ct-ct multiply equivalents" (keyswitch-bearing ops ~ 1)
W_ROT, W_CTMULT, W_PTMULT = 1.0, 1.0, 0.1

# depth (levels consumed) per layer per generated token -- stated component model:
# qkv proj 1, cache append masks 1, scores 1, softmax poly 6, AV 1, out proj 1,
# LN approx 2+2, MLP in 1, activation poly 4, MLP out 1  => 21
D_LAYER = 21
D_TOK = L * D_LAYER          # levels consumed per generated token (residual stream)
B_LEVELS = 18                # usable multiplicative budget between bootstraps
R_BOOT = 100.0               # [Verify] bootstrap / ct-mult cost ratio; HEonGPU's README
                             # reports ~99 ms slim bootstrapping (N=2^16, RTX 4090) vs
                             # ~1 ms-scale GPU ct-mults => order 1e2. Re-check per system.


def per_token_cost(t):
    """Op counts to decode ONE token when the cache already holds t-1 steps
    (so it holds t after the append).  Explicit loops over cache ciphertexts --
    the closed forms in the chapter are checked AGAINST this, not derived from it."""
    rot = ctm = ptm = 0.0
    m = math.ceil(t / G)                     # live cache ciphertexts per layer per K (and V)
    for _ in range(L):
        # -- cache-independent core (Halevi-Shoup/BSGS matvecs on one token vector)
        rot += 4 * 2 * math.ceil(math.sqrt(d));   ptm += 4 * d        # Wq,Wk,Wv,Wo
        rot += 2 * 2 * math.ceil(math.sqrt(dff)); ptm += 2 * dff     # MLP up/down
        ctm += H * (6 + 8)                       # softmax: exp poly + inverse poly, per head
        ctm += 6                                 # activation polynomial on FF ciphertext
        ctm += 2 * 8; rot += 2 * math.ceil(math.log2(d))  # 2 LayerNorm inv-sqrt approx
        # -- appending this step's K,V into the packed cache: rotate into slot + mask
        rot += 2; ptm += 2                       # one rotation + one mask mult, for K and V
        # -- attending over the packed cache: per cache ciphertext,
        #    scores: 1 ct-mult + log2(dk) rotations; AV: 1 ct-mult + log2(G) rotations
        for _ in range(m):
            ctm += 1; rot += math.ceil(math.log2(dk))
            ctm += 1; rot += math.ceil(math.log2(G))
    total = W_ROT * rot + W_CTMULT * ctm + W_PTMULT * ptm
    attend = L * m * (2 * W_CTMULT + W_ROT * (math.ceil(math.log2(dk)) + math.ceil(math.log2(G))))
    return dict(rot=rot, ctmult=ctm, ptmul=ptm, total=total, attend=attend, m=m)


def simulate(T):
    """Decode T tokens from an empty cache; greedy level accounting on the
    residual stream: bootstrap exactly when the budget is exhausted."""
    per_tok = np.zeros(T); attend = np.zeros(T); boots = np.zeros(T, dtype=int)
    levels, nboot = B_LEVELS, 0
    for i in range(T):
        c = per_token_cost(i + 1)
        per_tok[i], attend[i] = c["total"], c["attend"]
        b0 = nboot
        for _ in range(D_TOK):                # consume one level per depth unit
            if levels == 0:
                nboot += 1; levels = B_LEVELS  # bootstrap refreshes the budget
            levels -= 1
        boots[i] = nboot - b0
    return per_tok, attend, boots, nboot


if __name__ == "__main__":
    T = 1024
    per_tok, attend, boots, nboot = simulate(T)
    base = per_tok - attend                    # cache-independent share

    print("=== Artifact 41.1: encrypted-generation cost model (counting model) ===")
    print(f"geometry: L={L} d={d} H={H} dk={dk} | slots={SLOTS} G={G} steps/ct "
          f"| depth/token={D_TOK} budget={B_LEVELS} R_BOOT={R_BOOT} [Verify]")

    # ---- (1) per-token cost grows with cache length under this packing --------
    print("\n[1] per-token cost curve (ct-mult equivalents):")
    for t in [1, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024]:
        print(f"    t={t:5d}  total={per_tok[t-1]:9.1f}  attend={attend[t-1]:7.1f} "
              f"({100*attend[t-1]/per_tok[t-1]:4.1f}%)  cache cts m={math.ceil(t/G)}")
    diffs = np.diff(per_tok)
    assert (diffs >= -1e-9).all() and per_tok[-1] > per_tok[0], "cost must grow with t"
    # cross-check: attend cost is exactly linear in m; fit and print the residual
    ms = np.array([math.ceil(t / G) for t in range(1, T + 1)], float)
    slope, icept = np.polyfit(ms, attend, 1)
    resid = float(np.abs(np.polyval([slope, icept], ms) - attend).max())
    b_analytic = L * (2 + math.ceil(math.log2(dk)) + math.ceil(math.log2(G)))
    print(f"    fitted slope/cache-ct = {slope:.6f} vs analytic b = {b_analytic} "
          f"(max fit residual {resid:.2e})")
    assert abs(slope - b_analytic) < 1e-6 and resid < 1e-6

    # ---- (2) bootstraps match the analytic ceil accounting --------------------
    total_depth = T * D_TOK
    analytic = math.ceil(total_depth / B_LEVELS) - 1   # start fresh; none after last op
    nb_tok = D_TOK / B_LEVELS
    print(f"\n[2] bootstraps over T={T} tokens: simulated={nboot}  "
          f"analytic ceil({total_depth}/{B_LEVELS})-1={analytic}  "
          f"(={nb_tok:.1f}/token; tokens/bootstrap={1/nb_tok:.4f})")
    assert nboot == analytic, (nboot, analytic)
    # steady state is exactly D_TOK/B per token (B | D_TOK); token 1 gets the
    # fresh budget for free and needs one fewer
    assert (boots[1:] == int(nb_tok)).all() and boots[0] == int(nb_tok) - 1

    # ---- (3) crossover: growing attention cost overtakes bootstrap cost -------
    boot_cost = nb_tok * R_BOOT                 # per-token, constant in t (depth is)
    scan = next(t for t in range(1, T + 1) if attend[t - 1] > boot_cost)
    m_star = math.floor(boot_cost / b_analytic) + 1
    closed = (m_star - 1) * G + 1
    print(f"\n[3] per-token bootstrap cost = {boot_cost:.0f} mult-equivs; "
          f"attention overtakes it at t={scan} (closed form: m*={m_star}, t*={closed})")
    assert scan == closed, (scan, closed)
    print(f"    below t={closed}: bootstrapping outweighs cache attention; above: cache wins")
    print(f"    bootstrap share of total modeled time at t=1: "

Key Takeaways
  • Single-pass benchmarks structurally understate encrypted serving: decoding $T$ tokens multiplies depth by $T$, forces a ciphertext-resident KV cache, and inserts a secure argmax between every pair of passes. Certain
  • An encrypted KV cache costs $\approx 4\ell w$ bytes per cached scalar — a $\sim$200× expansion over fp16 at typical parameters — and appending under compact packing burns rotations, masks, and one level every token. Certain
  • Tokens per bootstrap is $B/d_{\text{tok}}$, which is $\ll 1$ for any realistic transformer (0.07 at the chapter's geometry); Theorem 41.1's $\lceil D/B\rceil - 1$ accounting matches the artifact's simulation exactly. Certain
  • The bootstrap-versus-cache crossover $t^{*} = \lfloor d_{\text{tok}}R/(Bb)\rfloor g + 1$ says GPU bootstrapping ($R\sim 10^2$) relocates the bottleneck to cache traffic rather than eliminating it; the constituent cost ratios drift with every library release. Verify
  • Published encrypted generation results are one-token-scale (PUMA's paper reports ~5 min/token for LLaMA-7B under MPC; Cachemir's reports <100 s/token for Llama-3-8B under FHE on GPU — different threat models, not comparable); long-horizon output quality under encryption is undemonstrated, and whether approximation error compounds across steps or is erased by argmax snapping remains genuinely unsettled.
