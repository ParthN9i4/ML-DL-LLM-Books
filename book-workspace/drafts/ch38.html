38.1 The Currency Change: What CKKS Charges For

The CKKS facts we need, stated once. A CKKS ciphertext over ring dimension $N$ holds $N/2$ complex slots and supports slotwise addition, slotwise multiplication, and cyclic rotation of the slot vector; arithmetic is approximate, carrying a noise floor that plays the role of Chapter 5's machine epsilon. Each multiplication roughly squares the encoded scale, and a rescaling divides it back down by dropping one prime from the ciphertext modulus chain — so a freshly encrypted ciphertext at level $\ell_{\max}$ can absorb only $\ell_{\max}$ sequential multiplications before the modulus is exhausted. Rotations and ciphertext-ciphertext multiplications additionally require a key-switching operation, which costs compute but no depth. When the levels run out, bootstrapping homomorphically re-encrypts the ciphertext to a higher level; it is the only way to refresh depth, and it is orders of magnitude more expensive than any other operation. That is the whole interface. For why any of it is true — RLWE, canonical embedding, relinearization — see the CKKS paper and a dedicated FHE text; nothing below depends on the internals.

Definition 38.1 (Multiplicative depth and the level budget)

The multiplicative depth of an arithmetic circuit is the maximum number of multiplications on any input-to-output path, counting both ciphertext–ciphertext and plaintext–ciphertext multiplications (each forces a rescale) and counting additions and rotations as zero. A CKKS parameter set fixes a level budget $\ell_{\max}$ at key generation: a circuit of depth $\le \ell_{\max}$ can be evaluated leveled, with no bootstrapping; deeper circuits cannot, no matter how long you are willing to wait. Depth is a budget, not a runtime.

38.1.1 The fee schedule and the budget's actual size

The resulting price list, in rough ascending order: additions are nearly free; plaintext-ciphertext multiplication is cheap in compute but costs one level; ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication costs one level plus a key-switch; a rotation costs zero levels but one key-switch, making it comparable in wall-clock to a ct-ct multiply; any non-polynomial function is not directly expressible at all; and bootstrapping costs seconds of CPU time and a slab of the budget itself. Security caps the budget. At 128-bit classical security with ternary secrets, the Homomorphic Encryption Standard's tables allow a total ciphertext modulus of at most 829 bits at $N=2^{15}$ and 1782 bits at $N=2^{16}$ Certain. With scaling primes of 40–50 bits plus auxiliary key-switching moduli, an $N=2^{16}$ parameter set yields on the order of 20–35 usable levels in total — and a bootstrappable configuration must reserve 10–15 of them for the bootstrapping circuit itself, leaving perhaps 10–20 levels of workspace between refreshes Verify: library- and configuration-dependent; check against OpenFHE's advanced-ckks-bootstrapping examples and Lattigo's bootstrapping parameters.

Bootstrapping cost is a moving number, so we cite reports rather than assert facts. Bossuat, Mouchet, Troncoso-Pastoriza, and Hubaux (EUROCRYPT 2021) report bootstrapping a full $\mathbb{C}^{32768}$-slot ciphertext in about 18 seconds in Lattigo on CPU, at 19.1 bits of mean output precision. Jung et al. (TCHES 2021) report a GPU implementation with amortized cost around 0.4 μs per plaintext bit, a claimed 257× over single-threaded CPU. Hardware accelerator papers project further orders of magnitude. The honest summary: seconds per ciphertext on a CPU, tens to hundreds of milliseconds on a well-engineered GPU stack, all figures tied to specific parameter sets and precision targets Verify: these numbers move yearly with hardware and library releases.

Two consequences frame everything else. First, the currency inversion: in plaintext, 99%+ of a transformer's FLOPs are matmuls (Chapter 24's $C \approx 6ND$ counted nothing else), and softmax and LayerNorm are noise. Under CKKS, matmuls are the one thing the scheme does well — plaintext weights, one level, rotations parallelizable — while every non-polynomial elementwise op detonates into polynomial evaluations and iterations that eat the level budget. Second, comparisons are ruinous: $\max$, $\mathrm{sign}$, argmax, and any data-dependent branch require high-degree polynomial approximations of a discontinuous function (Chapter 39 quantifies this), which is why the entire strategy space below is organized around avoiding them.

38.2 Threat Models: Who Is Hidden from Whom

There are three parties in the abstract deployment: the data owner (client), the model owner, and the compute provider (server); in the common two-party collapse the server owns both the model and the hardware. The pure-FHE, non-interactive model — the one this Part centers — has the client send encrypted activations; the server evaluates the whole network on ciphertext with plaintext weights and returns encrypted logits; the server learns nothing about the input beyond its shape, and because no data-dependent control flow is expressible, there is no side channel through access patterns: the circuit is identical for every input. The client learns only the output, not the weights. NEXUS (Zhang et al., ePrint 2024/136; NDSS 2025) and THOR (Moon et al., ePrint 2024/1881; CCS 2025) are this model; so are the polynomial-architecture line of Zimerman et al. (ICML 2024) and the encryption-friendly LLM architecture of Rho et al. (ICLR 2025). A variant hides the model too: if the model owner distrusts the compute provider, weights are also encrypted, and every plaintext-ciphertext matmul becomes ciphertext-ciphertext — same depth per multiply, but key-switches on every product and no precomputed plaintext diagonals, a large constant-factor loss Likely.

The main alternative is the interactive hybrid: HE (often additively or in low-depth bursts) for the linear layers, secret-sharing or garbled-circuit two-party computation for the non-polynomials. Iron (Hao et al., NeurIPS 2022), BOLT (Pang et al., IEEE S&P 2024), and BumbleBee (Lu et al., NDSS 2025) are this family, under semi-honest assumptions. The exchange rate flips again: comparisons become cheap (garbled circuits do $\max$ natively), depth stops mattering — but every nonlinearity costs communication rounds, both parties stay online, and the client does cryptographic work proportional to the model. BOLT reports a 10.91× communication reduction and 4.8–9.5× end-to-end speedup over the prior 2PC state of the art on BERT. A third pattern, client-aided HE, ships hard intermediate values back to the client for decryption: THE-X (Chen et al., Findings of ACL 2022) does this, which later work criticizes as both interactive and leaky, since decrypted intermediates reveal information about inputs and weights Likely.

38.3 The Non-Polynomial Inventory of a Transformer Block

Walk one pre-norm block, $y = x + \mathrm{MHA}(\mathrm{LN}_1(x))$, $z = y + \mathrm{MLP}(\mathrm{LN}_2(y))$, and flag everything CKKS cannot evaluate directly. LayerNorm: the mean is additions (fine), but $1/\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}+\epsilon}$ is a reciprocal square root — non-polynomial. The four weight matmuls and the two attention matmuls: polynomial, one level each — the good news. The softmax contains three separate offenses: the numerically stabilizing $\max$ subtraction of Chapter 5 is a comparison; $e^{s}$ is transcendental; and the normalization is a division. GELU is $x\,\Phi(x)$ with an erf inside — non-polynomial. Residual adds are free. At the output, argmax or nucleus sampling over the vocabulary is comparison-laden; in practice the server returns encrypted logits and the client decodes, so decoding exits the encrypted domain Certain.

Each offender has a strategy space with exactly four moves. (i) Polynomial approximation: replace $f$ by a minimax or least-squares polynomial on a bounded interval $[-B, B]$; degree $k$ costs $\lceil \log_2(k{+}1)\rceil$ levels and requires a range guarantee that inputs stay in the interval — a promise the model itself must be made to keep (Chapter 39). (ii) Iteration: Newton/Goldschmidt for $1/x$ and $1/\sqrt{x}$ — each iteration is itself a small polynomial, 2 levels, with quadratic convergence from a good initial guess on a known range. (iii) Architectural replacement: change the network so the operation never occurs — ReLU for GELU (THE-X), Gaussian-kernel attention for softmax (Rho et al.), power-function attention (Powerformer) — at the price of retraining or fine-tuning. (iv) Protocol escape: hand the operation to 2PC or the client, buying exactness with interaction. The comparison inside softmax is almost universally handled by move (iii) in disguise: drop the $\max$ shift and enforce bounded logits instead, which quietly couples cryptographic correctness to training-time regularization.

38.4 The Depth Ledger

Now the central accounting. Conventions, chosen to match standard practice and stated so the ledger is checkable: every plaintext-ciphertext or ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication costs one level; additions and rotations cost zero; a degree-$k$ polynomial evaluated by Paterson–Stockmeyer/BSGS costs $\lceil\log_2(k{+}1)\rceil$ levels ($k{=}15 \Rightarrow 4$); a Goldschmidt reciprocal iteration $y \leftarrow y(2 - ay)$ costs 2 levels, and the coupled Goldschmidt inverse-square-root iteration also costs 2 levels per iteration (3 multiplies, but only 2 on the critical path). These are the conventions under which published depth counts are exchanged Likely: papers vary in whether plaintext multiplies are charged; charging them is the conservative and more common choice. Settings: degree-15 polynomials for $\exp$ and GELU, $r=4$ Goldschmidt iterations for both reciprocal and rsqrt.

Read the ledger the way you read a profile. The attention sub-block costs 28 levels — the “roughly thirty” of this chapter's thesis — and the full block 44 (27/43 if the $1/\sqrt{d_k}$ scale is folded into $W_Q$, which any implementation should do). The six matmuls — all of the model's FLOPs, all of its parameters — account for 6 of 44 levels, under 14%. The two LayerNorms alone cost 20; softmax 13; GELU 4: the non-polynomials take 37 of 44 levels, 84% of the budget, for operations that were free in Part VI's accounting. And this ledger is charitable: it charges nothing for the masking multiplies that realistic packings need (see the pitfall below), nothing for level-alignment before residual adds, and it assumes the degree-15/4-iteration settings suffice for accuracy, which Chapter 39 will show is optimistic for LLM-scale ranges. Note also what the ledger does not depend on: $T$. Softmax is elementwise in the scores and the iterations are slotwise, so sequence length multiplies ciphertext counts and rotations, never depth.

38.5 Matmuls, Packings, and Rotation Counts

Depth is one axis; the other is rotations, because every rotation is a key-switch and key-switches dominate wall-clock in linear layers. Pack the activation matrix $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T\times d}$ row-major into slots: at $N=2^{16}$ there are $2^{15}$ slots per ciphertext, so BERT-base activations ($T{=}128$, $d{=}768$) occupy $\lceil Td/2^{15}\rceil = 3$ ciphertexts, with $2^{15}/d \approx 42$ token vectors sharing each ciphertext. A weight multiply $xW$ with $W$ plaintext then reduces to $d\times d$ matrix-vector products applied SIMD-fashion across the packed tokens. The classical method is Halevi and Shoup's diagonal decomposition (CRYPTO 2014): writing $\mathrm{diag}_i(W)[t] = W[t, (t{+}i)\bmod d]$,

$$xW \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{d-1} \mathrm{diag}_i(W) \odot \mathrm{rot}(x, i),$$

which is $d$ plaintext multiplies, $d-1$ rotations, $d-1$ additions — and only one level of depth, since the products are summed, not chained. The rotations, not the multiplies, are the cost: $d-1 = 767$ key-switches per packed matvec at $d=768$.

Theorem 38.1 (Baby-step–giant-step rotation count)

Let $d = n_1 n_2$. The diagonal-method product $xW$ can be computed with $n_1 + n_2 - 2$ ciphertext rotations; for square $d$ with $n_1=n_2=\sqrt d$ this is $2\sqrt d - 2$, versus $d-1$ for the naive method.

Proof. Write $i = jn_1 + k$ with $0 \le k < n_1$, $0 \le j < n_2$. Since $\mathrm{rot}(x, jn_1{+}k) = \mathrm{rot}(\mathrm{rot}(x,k), jn_1)$ and rotation distributes over $\odot$ with plaintext counter-rotation, $$xW = \sum_{j=0}^{n_2-1} \mathrm{rot}\!\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n_1-1} \mathrm{rot}\big(\mathrm{diag}_{jn_1+k}(W),\, -jn_1\big) \odot \mathrm{rot}(x,k),\; jn_1\Big).$$ The inner rotations of the diagonals act on plaintext and are free precomputation. The ciphertext rotations are the baby steps $\mathrm{rot}(x,k)$ for $k=1,\dots,n_1{-}1$ ($n_1{-}1$ of them, computed once and reused across all $j$) and the giant steps for $j=1,\dots,n_2{-}1$ ($n_2{-}1$ of them, applied to the inner sums). Total: $n_1+n_2-2$. $\blacksquare$

Example 38.1 (BSGS at $d=16$)

Naive diagonal method: $d-1 = 15$ rotations. BSGS with $n_1=n_2=4$: baby steps $\mathrm{rot}(x,1), \mathrm{rot}(x,2), \mathrm{rot}(x,3)$ — 3 rotations; then for each $j\in\{1,2,3\}$ one giant rotation by $4j$ of the inner sum $\sum_{k=0}^{3}\mathrm{rot}(\mathrm{diag}_{4j+k},-4j)\odot\mathrm{rot}(x,k)$ — 3 more. Total $3+3=6=2\sqrt{16}-2$, a $15/6 = 2.5\times$ saving. The artifact simulates exactly this decomposition at $d=256$ with counted np.roll calls: 255 rotations naive, 30 BSGS, and the two products agree with A @ v to a measured $4.3\times 10^{-14}$ and $1.8\times 10^{-14}$ respectively.

At transformer scale the factor matters: the cost model gives $d=768$: 767 naive vs. 54 BSGS ($n_1{=}24, n_2{=}32$; 14.2×), and $d=4096$: 4095 vs. $2\sqrt{4096}-2 = 126$ (32.5×) — the asymptotic $\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{d}$ advantage almost exactly. Halevi and Shoup's follow-up (CRYPTO 2018) adds hoisting — sharing the expensive decomposition step across many rotations of the same ciphertext — which cuts the constant further and is standard in HElib, Lattigo, and OpenFHE Certain.

The ciphertext-ciphertext matmuls $QK^\top$ and $PV$ are harder: the “matrix” side is encrypted, so there are no free plaintext diagonals. Jiang, Kim, Lauter, and Song (CCS 2018) give a constant-depth $d\times d$ ct-ct algorithm with $O(d)$ rotations; THOR reports a BSGS-organized ct-ct matmul and attributes to it a 9.7× speedup over Powerformer's on attention-shaped products. Our cost model uses a deliberately naive per-row model — per head, each of $T$ query rows costs a broadcast plus a $\log_2 d_k$ rotate-reduce — and even so the lesson is right: after BSGS collapses the weight-matmul rotations of a BERT-base block from 27,612 to 1,944, the attention ct-ct products' 39,936 rotations dominate the block at 41,880 total. Optimizing the easy matmuls exposes attention as the rotation bottleneck, which is why every serious system paper is, at its core, a $QK^\top$ packing paper Likely.

Common Pitfall

Treating slot rotation as np.roll on your logical vector. A real CKKS rotation is cyclic over all $N/2$ slots of the ciphertext. If 42 token vectors share one ciphertext and you rotate by $i$ to align diagonals for the matvec, each token's tail slots wrap into its neighbor's segment — silently, since CKKS raises no error and the result is just wrong in the boundary slots. The standard fix is a two-rotation-and-mask composition, and each mask is a plaintext multiply that costs a level: the ledger's “rotations are free” convention survives only if your packing keeps segments rotation-aligned (dimensions padded to powers of two, one logical block per rotation group). Every FHE matmul library has a version of this bug report.

38.6 Where the Budget Goes, and the Bootstrapping Conclusion

Assemble the ledger. One block: 44 levels, 84% of them non-polynomial. Twelve blocks (BERT-base): 528. Thirty-two blocks (a 7B-class LLM at $L=32$): 1,408 — against a leveled budget that tops out around 40 levels even at generous $N=2^{16}$ parameters. The 32-layer model overdraws the leveled budget by 35×: more than an order of magnitude, and no parameter tuning closes a gap of that shape, because the budget is capped by the security tables of 38.1 while the demand grows linearly in $L$. The conclusion is structural, not incidental: for any transformer deeper than about one block, bootstrapping is not an optimization to be avoided but the architecture itself. At a working figure of ~12 usable levels regained per refresh, the cost model schedules 41 bootstraps along a ciphertext lineage for the 12-layer model and 114 for the 32-layer — multiplied across every live ciphertext, which is why NEXUS treats bootstrap placement as a first-class optimization variable and why Rho et al. report their HE transformer's runtime dominated by bootstrapping even after architectural surgery Verify: bootstrap counts scale with the usable-levels-per-refresh figure, which is parameter- and library-specific.

This also fixes the exchange rate for Chapter 39's central trade: every extra degree of approximation accuracy costs depth, every unit of depth costs bootstraps, and every bootstrap costs seconds-to-milliseconds depending on hardware. Approximation error, multiplicative depth, and bootstrap count form a single coupled optimization — which is what the next chapter solves piece by piece, and what the system papers of Chapter 40 solve end to end.

Artifact 38.1 — A Transformer FHE Cost Model

A counting model — explicitly not an FHE implementation; no ciphertext is ever created — that prices a transformer config (L, d, heads, T, polynomial degrees, Goldschmidt iterations, slot count, packing) in the chapter's currency: multiplicative depth, ct-ct multiplication count, rotations under naive-diagonal and BSGS packings, and ciphertext counts. It verifies itself three ways: the block depth must equal the hand ledger of Section 38.4; the BSGS formula must match rotations counted one-by-one in a simulated Halevi–Shoup matvec (with np.roll standing in for slot rotation and the result checked against A @ v); and the scaling laws (linear in $L$, independent of $T$, ≥10× over a 40-level budget at $L{=}32$) are asserted, not assumed.

"""Artifact 38.1 -- A CKKS cost model for transformer inference.

This is a COUNTING MODEL, not an FHE implementation: it never encrypts
anything.  It charges every operation in the depth/rotation/ciphertext
currency of Chapter 38 and checks itself three ways:
  (1) the block depth matches a hand-computed ledger,
  (2) the BSGS rotation formula matches an actual simulated Halevi-Shoup
      matrix-vector product in NumPy (rotations counted one by one),
  (3) scaling laws (linear in L, T-independent depth) hold exactly.
Conventions (stated in Section 38.4): pt-ct mult = 1 level, ct-ct mult
= 1 level, rotation/addition = 0 levels; degree-k poly via
Paterson-Stockmeyer = ceil(log2(k+1)) levels; Goldschmidt reciprocal and
inverse-sqrt = 2 levels per iteration (2 resp. 3 ct-ct mults per iter).
"""
import numpy as np
from math import ceil, log2, isqrt

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- depth atoms
def poly_depth(k):        # Paterson-Stockmeyer depth for a degree-k poly
    return ceil(log2(k + 1))

def poly_mults(k):        # PS non-scalar mult count, ~ sqrt(2k) + log2 k
    return ceil((2 * k) ** 0.5) + max(0, ceil(log2(k + 1)) - 1)

REC_D, REC_M = 2, 2       # Goldschmidt reciprocal: depth 2, 2 muls / iter
RSQ_D, RSQ_M = 2, 3       # coupled Goldschmidt rsqrt: depth 2, 3 muls / iter

# ------------------------------------------------------------------ the ledger
def block_ledger(cfg):
    """Per-block (label, levels) list for one pre-norm transformer block."""
    r, kx, kg = cfg["newton_iters"], cfg["exp_deg"], cfg["gelu_deg"]
    ln = [("  LayerNorm: variance (ct-ct square)", 1),
          ("  LayerNorm: rsqrt, %d Goldschmidt iters" % r, RSQ_D * r),
          ("  LayerNorm: apply (x-mu) * rsqrt (ct-ct)", 1)]
    attn = ln + [
        ("  Q,K,V projections (pt-ct matmul)", 1),
        ("  scale by 1/sqrt(d_k) (pt mult, foldable)", 1),
        ("  Q K^T (ct-ct matmul)", 1),
        ("  softmax: exp poly, degree %d" % kx, poly_depth(kx)),
        ("  softmax: reciprocal, %d Goldschmidt iters" % r, REC_D * r),
        ("  softmax: normalize (ct-ct)", 1),
        ("  attention @ V (ct-ct matmul)", 1),
        ("  output projection W_O (pt-ct matmul)", 1)]
    mlp = ln + [
        ("  W_up (pt-ct matmul)", 1),
        ("  GELU poly, degree %d" % kg, poly_depth(kg)),
        ("  W_down (pt-ct matmul)", 1)]
    return attn, mlp

def block_depth(cfg):
    attn, mlp = block_ledger(cfg)
    return sum(v for _, v in attn), sum(v for _, v in mlp)

def total_depth(cfg):                      # residuals/rotations cost 0 levels
    a, m = block_depth(cfg)
    return cfg["L"] * (a + m)

# ------------------------------------------------------- rotations & ciphertexts
def rot_naive(d):                          # Halevi-Shoup: one rot per diagonal
    return d - 1

def rot_bsgs(d):                           # n1 baby + n2 giant, n1*n2 = d
    n1 = isqrt(d)
    while d % n1:                          # largest factor <= sqrt(d)
        n1 -= 1
    return n1 + d // n1 - 2                # = 2*sqrt(d) - 2 when d is square

def block_costs(cfg, packing="bsgs"):
    """Rotations, ct-ct mults, and ciphertext counts for ONE block."""
    d, H, T, s = cfg["d"], cfg["heads"], cfg["T"], cfg["slots"]
    dk, r = d // H, cfg["newton_iters"]
    n_x = ceil(T * d / s)                  # activation ciphertexts
    n_h = ceil(T * 4 * d / s)              # MLP hidden ciphertexts
    n_s = ceil(H * T * T / s)              # attention-score ciphertexts
    n_kv = ceil(T * dk / s)                # one head's K (or V) ciphertexts
    rot1 = {"naive": rot_naive, "bsgs": rot_bsgs}[packing](d)
    # 12 d x d pt-ct matmul equivalents: Wq,Wk,Wv,Wo + 4 for W_up + 4 for W_down
    rot_w = 12 * n_x * rot1
    # ct-ct matmuls, naive per-row model: each of T query rows per head needs a
    # log2(T) broadcast and a log2(dk) rotate-reduce; same under either packing.
    rot_cc = 2 * H * T * (ceil(log2(T)) + ceil(log2(dk)))
    mults = (2 * (n_x + RSQ_M * r + n_x)             # two LayerNorms
             + H * T * n_kv                          # Q K^T
             + poly_mults(cfg["exp_deg"]) * n_s      # exp on scores
             + REC_M * r + n_s                       # reciprocal + normalize
             + H * T * n_kv                          # attn @ V
             + poly_mults(cfg["gelu_deg"]) * n_h)    # GELU on hidden
    return dict(rot=rot_w + rot_cc, rot_weights=rot_w, rot_cc=rot_cc,
                ctct_mults=mults, cts=dict(acts=n_x, scores=n_s, hidden=n_h))

# --------------------------------------- simulate Halevi-Shoup to verify counts
def hs_matvec(A, v, bsgs=False):
    """Diagonal-method A @ v with np.roll standing in for slot rotation.
    Returns (result, rotations actually performed). Diagonal extraction and
    plaintext pre-rotations are free (they happen on plaintext)."""
    d = len(v); rots = 0
    diag = lambda i: np.array([A[t, (t + i) % d] for t in range(d)])
    if not bsgs:
        acc = diag(0) * v
        for i in range(1, d):
            rots += 1                              # rotate v by i (hoisted: 1 ea.)
            acc = acc + diag(i) * np.roll(v, -i)
        return acc, rots
    n1 = isqrt(d); n2 = d // n1; assert n1 * n2 == d
    baby = [v]
    for k in range(1, n1):
        rots += 1
        baby.append(np.roll(v, -k))                # n1 - 1 baby rotations
    acc = np.zeros(d)
    for j in range(n2):

                    for k in range(n1))            # plaintext pre-rotation: free
        if j:
            rots += 1                              # n2 - 1 giant rotations
            inner = np.roll(inner, -j * n1)
        acc = acc + inner
    return acc, rots

Key Takeaways
  • CKKS re-prices the transformer: matmuls are cheap (one level, rotations parallelizable), non-polynomials are expensive, comparisons are ruinous, and depth is a budget fixed at key generation — capped near 40 levels by the 128-bit security tables at $N=2^{16}$. Certain
  • One pre-norm attention sub-block costs 28 multiplicative levels at degree-15 polynomials and 4 Goldschmidt iterations, the full block 44 — and 84% of that goes to LayerNorm, softmax, and GELU, inverting the plaintext cost profile where those operations were free. Certain (given the stated conventions)
  • Baby-step–giant-step cuts the Halevi–Shoup diagonal matvec from $d-1$ to $n_1+n_2-2 \approx 2\sqrt d$ rotations — 4,095 to 126 at $d=4096$ — after which the ciphertext-ciphertext attention matmuls dominate the rotation bill. Certain
  • Depth grows linearly in $L$ and is independent of $T$; a 32-layer model needs ~1,400 levels against a ~40-level leveled budget, so bootstrapping — seconds on CPU, tens-to-hundreds of milliseconds on GPU per ciphertext, per current reports — is the architecture, not an optimization. Verify (bootstrap costs move with hardware and library)
  • Whether pure-FHE or interactive HE+MPC hybrids are the right road is unsettled: hybrids report better latency on LAN benchmarks, pure FHE needs no client liveness or per-query bandwidth; published latencies are not cross-comparable, and this book bets on non-interactivity for serving. Contested
